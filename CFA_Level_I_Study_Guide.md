- **Duration**: 4 hours 30 minutes total (2h 15min per session)
- **Question Type**: Multiple choice (A, B, or C)
- **Computer-Based Testing**: Administered at Prometric test centers
- **Scoring**: Questions are equally weighted

### Level I Focus
Level I emphasizes **foundational knowledge**, **tools**, and **inputs** for investment analysis. It tests:
- Basic concepts and terminology
- Understanding of fundamental tools and techniques
- Introduction to asset valuation and portfolio management
- Financial reporting and analysis fundamentals

- Scenario-based questions require application, not memorization

2. Place **integrity of profession** and **clients' interests** above own
3. Use reasonable care and exercise independent professional judgment
4. Practice and encourage others to practice in a professional and ethical manner
5. Promote integrity and viability of global capital markets
6. Maintain and improve professional competence

## Topic 2: Quantitative Methods (6-9%)

### Overview
Quantitative Methods provides the mathematical and statistical foundation for financial analysis. 12 learning modules covering rates of return, time value of money, statistics, probability, hypothesis testing, and regression.

### Key Topics

#### A. Time Value of Money (TVM)
**Core Concepts:**
- Future Value (FV): FV = PV × (1 + r)^n
- Present Value (PV): PV = FV / (1 + r)^n
- Annuities (ordinary and annuity due)
- Perpetuities: PV = PMT / r
- Uneven cash flows

**Applications:**
- Bond and stock valuation
- Loan and mortgage calculations
- Investment project analysis

#### B. Statistical Concepts

**Measures of Central Tendency:**
- **Arithmetic Mean**: Sum of observations / number of observations
- **Median**: Middle value when data is ordered
- **Mode**: Most frequently occurring value
- **Geometric Mean**: Used for investment returns over multiple periods
- **Harmonic Mean**: Used for ratios and rates

**Measures of Dispersion:**
- **Range**: Maximum - Minimum
- **Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)**
- **Variance**: σ² = Σ(Xi - μ)² / N
- **Standard Deviation**: σ = √variance
- **Coefficient of Variation**: CV = s / X̄ (measure of relative dispersion)

**Measures of Shape:**
- **Skewness**: Measures asymmetry of distribution
  - Positive skew: Long right tail (mean > median > mode)
  - Negative skew: Long left tail (mean < median < mode)
- **Kurtosis**: Measures "fatness" of tails
  - Excess kurtosis > 0: Leptokurtic (fat tails)
  - Excess kurtosis < 0: Platykurtic (thin tails)

#### C. Probability Concepts
- **Probability rules**: Addition rule, multiplication rule
- **Conditional probability**: P(A|B) = P(AB) / P(B)
- **Expected value**: E(X) = Σ[P(Xi) × Xi]
- **Variance of random variable**: σ² = E(X²) - [E(X)]²
- **Covariance**: Cov(X,Y) = E[(X - E(X))(Y - E(Y))]
- **Correlation**: ρ(X,Y) = Cov(X,Y) / (σX × σY)

**Probability Distributions:**
- **Discrete distributions**: Binomial, Bernoulli
- **Continuous distributions**: Normal, lognormal, Student's t, Chi-square, F-distribution

#### D. Sampling and Estimation
- **Sampling methods**: Simple random, stratified, cluster
- **Central Limit Theorem**: Sample means are normally distributed for large samples
- **Standard error**: SE = σ / √n
- **Confidence intervals**: Point estimate ± (critical value × standard error)

#### E. Hypothesis Testing
**Framework:**
1. State null hypothesis (H₀) and alternative hypothesis (Ha)
2. Select test statistic and significance level (α)
3. Collect data and calculate test statistic
4. Make statistical decision (reject or fail to reject H₀)
5. Make economic/investment decision

**Types of Errors:**
- **Type I Error**: Rejecting true null hypothesis (α = significance level)
- **Type II Error**: Failing to reject false null hypothesis (β)
- **Power of test**: 1 - β (probability of correctly rejecting false H₀)

**Common Tests:**
- **Z-test**: When population variance is known, large sample
- **t-test**: When population variance is unknown, small sample
- **Chi-square test**: Test for independence, goodness of fit
- **F-test**: Compare variances of two populations

#### F. Simple Linear Regression
- **Regression equation**: Y = b₀ + b₁X + ε
- **Interpretation**: b₁ is slope (change in Y for one-unit change in X)
- **Coefficient of determination (R²)**: Proportion of variance explained
- **Assumptions**: Linearity, independence, homoskedasticity, normality
- **ANOVA**: Analysis of variance for regression

#### G. Big Data and Machine Learning (Introduction)
- Overview of AI and machine learning in finance
- Data mining and pattern recognition
- Supervised vs. unsupervised learning
- High-level concepts only at Level I

### Formula Sheet Highlights
- TVM: FV, PV, annuities, perpetuities
- Statistics: Mean, variance, standard deviation, CV, skewness, kurtosis
- Probability: Expected value, variance, covariance, correlation
- Hypothesis testing: Test statistics (z, t, chi-square, F)
- Regression: Slope, intercept, R², standard error

### Study Tips
1. **Master your calculator** - TI BA II Plus or HP 12C
2. **Practice problems extensively** - QM requires application
3. **Focus on conceptual understanding** - Not just formula memorization
4. **Understand when to use each test** - Know assumptions and conditions
5. **Create formula cards** - Quick reference for common equations

## Topic 3: Economics (6-9%)

### Overview
Economics provides the framework for understanding how markets function, economic indicators, and how economic factors affect investment decisions. Divided into microeconomics, macroeconomics, and international economics.

### Key Topics

#### A. Microeconomics

**Demand and Supply Analysis:**
- **Law of Demand**: Inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded
- **Law of Supply**: Direct relationship between price and quantity supplied
- **Market equilibrium**: Where demand = supply
- **Elasticity**:
  - Price elasticity of demand = %ΔQd / %ΔP
  - Elastic (|E| > 1), Inelastic (|E| < 1), Unit elastic (|E| = 1)
- **Consumer surplus and producer surplus**

**Theory of the Firm:**
- **Production functions**: Relationship between inputs and output
- **Cost curves**: Fixed, variable, total, average, marginal costs
- **Profit maximization**: Where MR = MC
- **Revenue**: Total, average, marginal revenue
- **Economies and diseconomies of scale**

**Market Structures:**
1. **Perfect Competition**
   - Many buyers and sellers
   - Homogeneous products
   - Price takers
   - Long-run economic profit = 0

2. **Monopolistic Competition**
   - Many sellers, differentiated products
   - Some pricing power
   - Low barriers to entry

3. **Oligopoly**
   - Few large firms
   - Interdependent pricing decisions
   - High barriers to entry

4. **Monopoly**
   - Single seller
   - Price maker
   - High barriers to entry
   - Potential for economic profits

#### B. Macroeconomics

**Aggregate Output and Income:**
- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**:
  - Expenditure approach: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)
  - Income approach: Sum of all incomes
- **Real vs. Nominal GDP**
- **GDP deflator**: Price level indicator
- **Economic growth** and business cycles

**Aggregate Demand and Supply:**
- **AD curve**: Relationship between price level and real output demanded
- **AS curves**: Short-run and long-run aggregate supply
- **Shifts in AD/AS**: Impact on output and price level

**Inflation:**
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** and other measures
- **Types**: Demand-pull, cost-push, built-in
- **Effects**: Purchasing power, real returns, uncertainty
- **Deflation and disinflation**

**Unemployment:**
- **Types**: Frictional, structural, cyclical
- **Natural rate of unemployment**: Frictional + structural
- **Full employment** vs. actual unemployment

**Monetary Policy:**
- **Central bank functions**: Control money supply, lender of last resort
- **Tools**:
  - Open market operations
  - Discount rate
  - Reserve requirements
- **Expansionary vs. Contractionary policy**
- **Money creation process** and money multiplier

**Fiscal Policy:**
- **Government spending and taxation**
- **Budget deficits and surpluses**
- **Automatic stabilizers**
- **Expansionary vs. Contractionary fiscal policy**

**Economic Growth:**
- **Factors**: Labor, capital, technology (TFP)
- **Production function**: Y = A × f(K, L)
- **Sustainable growth rate**

#### C. International Economics

**International Trade:**
- **Comparative advantage**: Ricardo's theory
- **Absolute advantage**
- **Trade restrictions**: Tariffs, quotas, subsidies
- **Benefits of free trade**

**Exchange Rates:**
- **Spot rates and forward rates**
- **Appreciation and depreciation**
- **Factors affecting exchange rates**:
  - Interest rate differentials
  - Inflation rate differentials
  - Economic growth
  - Government intervention

**Currency Exchange Rate Systems:**
- **Floating exchange rates**
- **Fixed exchange rates**
- **Managed float**

**Balance of Payments:**
- **Current account**: Trade balance, income, transfers
- **Capital and financial account**: Investment flows
- **BOP identity**: Current account + Capital account = 0

### Study Tips
1. **Understand relationships, not just definitions**
2. **Use graphs**: Supply/demand, AD/AS, cost curves
3. **Connect micro and macro concepts**
4. **Apply concepts to real-world scenarios**
5. **Don't just memorize** - understand the economic logic

## Topic 4: Financial Statement Analysis (11-14%)

### Overview
FSA is one of the **most important and heavily weighted topics** at Level I. It provides tools to analyze financial statements, assess financial health, and make informed investment decisions.

### Key Topics

#### A. Financial Reporting Standards

**IFRS vs. US GAAP:**
- **IFRS**: Principles-based, used in 100+ countries
- **US GAAP**: Rules-based, used in United States
- **Key differences**: Inventory (LIFO), development costs, reversals of impairments

**Standard-Setting Bodies:**
- **IASB**: International Accounting Standards Board (IFRS)
- **FASB**: Financial Accounting Standards Board (US GAAP)
- **IOSCO**: International Organization of Securities Commissions

**Framework Concepts:**
- **Accrual accounting** vs. cash accounting
- **Going concern assumption**
- **Qualitative characteristics**: Relevance, faithful representation
- **Elements**: Assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses

#### B. Financial Statement Analysis Framework

**Six Steps:**
1. **Define purpose and context**
2. **Collect data**
3. **Process the data**
4. **Analyze and interpret the data**
5. **Develop conclusions and recommendations**
6. **Follow-up and update**

#### C. Income Statement Analysis

**Key Components:**
- **Revenue recognition**: When and how much to recognize
- **Expense recognition**: Matching principle
- **Gross profit = Revenue - COGS**
- **Operating profit (EBIT)**: Earnings before interest and taxes
- **Net income**: Bottom line

**Non-Recurring Items:**
- **Discontinued operations**
- **Extraordinary items** (rare under IFRS/GAAP)
- **Unusual or infrequent items**

**Earnings Per Share (EPS):**
- **Basic EPS** = (Net Income - Preferred Dividends) / Weighted Avg Shares
- **Diluted EPS**: Includes effect of convertible securities, options

#### D. Balance Sheet Analysis

**Assets:**
- **Current assets**: Cash, receivables, inventory
- **Non-current assets**: PP&E, intangibles, investments

**Liabilities:**
- **Current liabilities**: Payables, short-term debt
- **Non-current liabilities**: Long-term debt, deferred taxes, pensions

**Equity:**
- **Common stock**
- **Retained earnings**
- **Other comprehensive income**

**Balance Sheet Equation:**
Assets = Liabilities + Equity

#### E. Cash Flow Statement Analysis

**Three Sections:**
1. **Operating Activities (CFO)**
   - Direct method vs. indirect method
   - Start with net income (indirect)
   - Adjust for non-cash items and working capital changes

2. **Investing Activities (CFI)**
   - Purchase/sale of long-term assets
   - Capital expenditures (CapEx)

3. **Financing Activities (CFF)**
   - Debt issuance/repayment
   - Equity issuance
   - Dividends paid

**Free Cash Flow:**
- **FCFF (Free Cash Flow to the Firm)** = CFO + Int(1-T) - FCInv
- **FCFE (Free Cash Flow to Equity)** = CFO - FCInv + Net Borrowing

#### F. Financial Ratios

**Liquidity Ratios:**
- **Current Ratio** = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
- **Quick Ratio** = (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities
- **Cash Ratio** = Cash / Current Liabilities

**Solvency Ratios (Leverage):**
- **Debt-to-Equity** = Total Debt / Total Equity
- **Debt-to-Assets** = Total Debt / Total Assets
- **Interest Coverage** = EBIT / Interest Expense
- **Fixed Charge Coverage** = (EBIT + Lease Payments) / (Interest + Lease Payments)

**Profitability Ratios:**
- **Gross Profit Margin** = Gross Profit / Revenue
- **Operating Profit Margin** = Operating Income / Revenue
- **Net Profit Margin** = Net Income / Revenue
- **ROA (Return on Assets)** = Net Income / Average Total Assets
- **ROE (Return on Equity)** = Net Income / Average Shareholders' Equity

**DuPont Analysis:**
ROE = (Net Income / Sales) × (Sales / Assets) × (Assets / Equity)
ROE = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier

**Activity Ratios:**
- **Inventory Turnover** = COGS / Average Inventory
- **Days Inventory on Hand (DOH)** = 365 / Inventory Turnover
- **Receivables Turnover** = Revenue / Average Receivables
- **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** = 365 / Receivables Turnover
- **Payables Turnover** = Purchases / Average Payables
- **Days Payables Outstanding (DPO)** = 365 / Payables Turnover
- **Asset Turnover** = Revenue / Average Total Assets

**Cash Conversion Cycle:**
CCC = DOH + DSO - DPO

#### G. Inventory Analysis
- **FIFO (First-In, First-Out)**
- **LIFO (Last-In, First-Out)** - Not allowed under IFRS
- **Weighted Average Cost**
- **Impact on financial statements during inflation**

#### H. Long-Lived Assets
- **Capitalization vs. expensing**
- **Depreciation methods**: Straight-line, declining balance, units of production
- **Impairment**: Test and recognize losses
- **Intangible assets**: Amortization vs. no amortization (indefinite life)

#### I. Income Taxes
- **Current tax expense**
- **Deferred tax assets and liabilities**
- **Effective tax rate vs. statutory tax rate**

#### J. Financial Statement Quality
- **High quality**: Conservative, sustainable, adequate returns
- **Earnings quality**: Accruals-based vs. cash-based earnings
- **Red flags**: Aggressive revenue recognition, poor cash flow, frequent changes

### Study Tips
1. **Practice with real financial statements**
2. **Master ratio calculations and interpretations**
3. **Understand adjustments for comparability**
4. **Know differences between IFRS and US GAAP**
5. **Lots of EOC questions** - FSA has many practice problems

## Topic 5: Corporate Issuers (6-9%)

### Overview
Corporate Issuers (formerly Corporate Finance) covers how companies make investment and financing decisions, manage working capital, and implement governance structures.

### Key Topics

#### A. Corporate Governance
- **Stakeholder relationships**: Shareholders, board, management, creditors
- **Principal-agent problems**: Conflicts of interest
- **Board responsibilities**: Oversight, strategic direction
- **ESG factors**: Environmental, social, governance considerations

#### B. Capital Budgeting

**Project Analysis:**
- **Net Present Value (NPV)**: Sum of PV of all cash flows
  - Accept if NPV > 0
- **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**: Discount rate where NPV = 0
  - Accept if IRR > required return
- **Payback Period**: Time to recover initial investment
- **Profitability Index (PI)**: PV of future CFs / Initial Investment

**NPV vs. IRR:**
- NPV preferred when conflicts arise
- IRR issues: Multiple IRRs, scale problem, timing problem

**Real Options:**
- Timing options
- Abandonment options
- Expansion options
- Flexibility options

#### C. Cost of Capital

**Cost of Debt:**
- rd = YTM on company's debt × (1 - Tax Rate)
- After-tax cost due to interest tax shield

**Cost of Preferred Stock:**
- rp = Dp / P0

**Cost of Common Equity:**
- **CAPM**: re = Rf + β(Rm - Rf)
- **Dividend Discount Model**: re = (D1 / P0) + g
- **Bond Yield Plus Risk Premium**: re = rd + Risk Premium

**Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC):**
WACC = (wd × rd × (1-T)) + (wp × rp) + (we × re)

Where:
- wd, wp, we = weights of debt, preferred, equity (at market values)
- T = marginal tax rate

#### D. Capital Structure
- **Modigliani-Miller (M&M) Propositions**: With/without taxes
- **Trade-off theory**: Balance tax benefits vs. financial distress costs
- **Pecking order theory**: Prefer internal financing, then debt, then equity
- **Optimal capital structure**: Minimizes WACC, maximizes firm value

#### E. Leverage

**Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL):**
DOL = % Change in EBIT / % Change in Sales

**Degree of Financial Leverage (DFL):**
DFL = % Change in EPS / % Change in EBIT

**Degree of Total Leverage (DTL):**
DTL = DOL × DFL

**Breakeven Analysis:**
Breakeven Quantity = Fixed Costs / (Price - Variable Cost per Unit)

#### F. Working Capital Management
- **Operating cycle and cash conversion cycle**
- **Managing inventory, receivables, payables**
- **Short-term financing options**
- **Liquidity management**

### Study Tips
1. **Understand NPV and IRR thoroughly** - Key concepts
2. **Master WACC calculation** - Appears frequently
3. **Practice leverage problems**
4. **Connect to other topics**: Uses QM (TVM), Portfolio (CAPM), FSA (ratios)
5. **Qualitative concepts**: Don't focus only on calculations

## Topic 6: Equity Investments (11-14%)

### Overview
Equity Investments covers stock market organization, equity securities, indices, valuation, and market efficiency. This is one of the three "big" Level I topics.

### Key Topics

#### A. Market Organization and Structure

**Types of Markets:**
- **Primary markets**: New issue sales (IPOs, SEOs)
- **Secondary markets**: Trading of existing securities

**Market Structures:**
- **Quote-driven markets**: Dealers provide quotes (bid-ask)
- **Order-driven markets**: Orders from public match directly
- **Brokered markets**: Brokers find counterparties

**Order Types:**
- **Market orders**: Execute immediately at best price
- **Limit orders**: Execute at specified price or better
- **Stop orders**: Become market orders when stop price reached

**Market Efficiency:**
- **Transaction costs**: Commissions, bid-ask spread
- **Market impact**: Price movement from large orders

#### B. Security Market Indices

**Types of Indices:**
- **Price-weighted**: Sum of stock prices / divisor (e.g., DJIA)
- **Value-weighted (market-cap)**: Weight by market capitalization (e.g., S&P 500)
- **Equal-weighted**: Each stock gets equal weight

**Index Construction:**
- Constituent selection criteria
- Weighting methods
- Rebalancing frequency

#### C. Market Efficiency

**Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH):**

1. **Weak-form efficiency**
   - Prices reflect all past market data
   - Technical analysis cannot earn abnormal returns

2. **Semi-strong-form efficiency**
   - Prices reflect all publicly available information
   - Fundamental analysis cannot earn abnormal returns

3. **Strong-form efficiency**
   - Prices reflect all information (public and private)
   - Even insider information cannot earn abnormal returns

**Market Anomalies:**
- Calendar effects
- Momentum effects
- Value vs. growth
- Size effects

#### D. Equity Valuation Models

**Dividend Discount Model (DDM):**

**Gordon Growth Model** (constant growth):
V0 = D1 / (r - g)

Where:
- D1 = Expected dividend next period
- r = Required return
- g = Constant growth rate

**Multi-stage DDM**: Different growth rates over time

**Preferred Stock Valuation** (perpetuity):
V0 = D / r

**Free Cash Flow Models:**

**FCFE Model:**
V0 = FCFE1 / (r - g)

**Price Multiples:**
- **P/E Ratio** (Price-to-Earnings)
- **P/B Ratio** (Price-to-Book)
- **P/S Ratio** (Price-to-Sales)
- **Dividend Yield** = Annual Dividend / Price
- **EV/EBITDA**: Enterprise Value to EBITDA

**Justified P/E Ratio** (from Gordon Growth Model):
P0/E1 = (1 - b) / (r - g)

Where b = earnings retention rate

#### E. Industry and Company Analysis

**Porter's Five Forces:**
1. Threat of new entrants
2. Threat of substitutes
3. Bargaining power of buyers
4. Bargaining power of suppliers
5. Rivalry among existing competitors

**Industry Life Cycle:**
- Embryonic
- Growth
- Shakeout
- Mature
- Decline

#### F. Equity Securities

**Common Stock:**
- Voting rights
- Residual claim on assets
- Dividends (not guaranteed)

**Preferred Stock:**
- Fixed dividend
- Priority over common stock
- Usually no voting rights
- Callable, putable, convertible features

**Depository Receipts:**
- **ADR (American Depositary Receipt)**
- **GDR (Global Depositary Receipt)**

### Study Tips
1. **Master valuation models** - DDM, P/E multiples
2. **Understand market efficiency** - All three forms
3. **Practice valuation problems** - Many calculations
4. **Know market structures** - How markets operate
5. **Link to other topics**: Uses QM (statistics), Corporate Finance (dividends)

## Topic 7: Fixed Income (11-14%)

### Overview
Fixed Income is one of the three "big" Level I topics alongside FSA and Equity. It covers bond characteristics, pricing, yield measures, and credit analysis.

### Key Topics

#### A. Fixed Income Basics

**Bond Features:**
- **Par value (face value)**: Amount paid at maturity
- **Coupon rate**: Annual interest rate
- **Maturity date**: When principal is repaid
- **Call provisions**: Issuer can redeem early
- **Put provisions**: Holder can sell back to issuer

**Bond Types:**
- **Fixed-rate bonds**: Constant coupon
- **Floating-rate notes (FRNs)**: Variable coupon (e.g., LIBOR + spread)
- **Zero-coupon bonds**: No periodic coupons
- **Inflation-linked bonds**: Principal/coupons adjusted for inflation

#### B. Bond Pricing and Yields

**Bond Pricing Formula:**
Price = Σ[Ct / (1+r)^t] + [FV / (1+r)^N]

Where:
- Ct = Coupon payment at time t
- FV = Face value
- r = Discount rate (yield)
- N = Number of periods

**Price-Yield Relationship:**
- **Inverse relationship**: When yields ↑, prices ↓
- When coupon rate > YTM: Bond trades at **premium** (Price > Par)
- When coupon rate < YTM: Bond trades at **discount** (Price < Par)
- When coupon rate = YTM: Bond trades at **par** (Price = Par)

**Yield Measures:**

1. **Yield to Maturity (YTM)**
   - IRR of bond's cash flows
   - Assumes reinvestment at YTM

2. **Current Yield**
   - Annual Coupon / Price

3. **Yield to Call (YTC)**
   - YTM if bond is called at first call date

4. **Yield to Worst**
   - Lowest yield among YTM, YTC, and other scenarios

**Spot Rates and Forward Rates:**
- **Spot rate**: Yield on zero-coupon bond
- **Forward rate**: Implied future interest rate
- Relationship: (1 + S2)² = (1 + S1)(1 + f1,1)

#### C. Interest Rate Risk

**Duration:**
- **Macaulay Duration**: Weighted average time to receive cash flows
- **Modified Duration**: Measures price sensitivity to yield changes
  - %ΔPrice ≈ -Modified Duration × ΔYield
- **Effective Duration**: For bonds with embedded options

**Duration Properties:**
- Longer maturity → Higher duration
- Lower coupon → Higher duration
- Lower YTM → Higher duration

**Convexity:**
- Measures curvature of price-yield relationship
- Positive convexity: Price increases more when yields fall than it decreases when yields rise
- %ΔPrice ≈ (-Modified Duration × ΔYield) + (½ × Convexity × (ΔYield)²)

**Dollar Duration:**
Dollar Duration = Modified Duration × Price × 0.01

#### D. Credit Analysis

**Credit Risk Components:**
- **Default risk**: Probability of default
- **Loss severity**: Loss given default (LGD)
- **Expected loss** = Probability of Default × Loss Given Default

**Credit Ratings:**
- **Investment grade**: AAA to BBB (S&P) or Aaa to Baa (Moody's)
- **Non-investment grade (high yield, junk bonds)**: BB and below (S&P) or Ba and below (Moody's)

**Credit Spread:**
- Yield differential between corporate bond and government bond
- Compensates for credit risk, liquidity risk

**The Four Cs of Credit Analysis:**
1. **Capacity**: Ability to repay (cash flows, earnings)
2. **Collateral**: Assets securing the debt
3. **Covenants**: Bond terms protecting lenders
4. **Character**: Management quality and integrity

#### E. Bond Structures

**Securitization:**
- **Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS)**: Backed by mortgages
- **Asset-Backed Securities (ABS)**: Backed by other assets (auto loans, credit cards)
- **Collateralized Debt Obligations (CDOs)**

**Prepayment Risk:**
- Homeowners may refinance when rates fall
- Reduces expected life of MBS

**Seniority and Security:**
- **Secured debt**: Backed by collateral
- **Unsecured debt (debentures)**: No specific collateral
- **Senior debt**: Priority in bankruptcy
- **Subordinated debt**: Lower priority

### Study Tips
1. **Master bond pricing** - Fundamental skill
2. **Understand duration thoroughly** - Key risk measure
3. **Practice yield calculations** - Calculator efficiency important
4. **Know credit analysis framework** - Both quantitative and qualitative
5. **Memorize relationships**: Price-yield, duration-maturity-coupon

## Topic 8: Derivatives (5-8%)

### Overview
Derivatives covers forwards, futures, options, and swaps. While lower weighted than other topics, it's important to understand the mechanics and uses of derivative instruments.

### Key Topics

#### A. Derivative Basics

**Definition:**
A derivative is a financial instrument whose value is derived from the value of an underlying asset.

**Underlying Assets:**
- Equities
- Fixed income securities
- Currencies
- Commodities
- Interest rates
- Credit

**Uses of Derivatives:**
- **Hedging**: Reduce risk
- **Speculation**: Take directional bets
- **Arbitrage**: Exploit pricing discrepancies

#### B. Forward Commitments

**Forward Contracts:**
- **Over-the-counter (OTC)**: Customized, bilateral agreement
- **Obligation** to buy/sell at specified price on future date
- **Counterparty risk**: Risk of default
- **No upfront payment** (usually)

**Forward Contract Payoffs:**
- **Long forward**: Payoff = ST - F0
- **Short forward**: Payoff = F0 - ST

Where ST = Spot price at maturity, F0 = Forward price

**Futures Contracts:**
- **Exchange-traded**: Standardized contracts
- **Daily settlement (marking to market)**: Gains/losses settled daily
- **Clearinghouse**: Reduces counterparty risk
- **Margin requirements**: Initial and maintenance margin

**Key Differences: Forwards vs. Futures**
| Feature | Forward | Futures |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Trading | OTC | Exchange |
| Standardization | Customized | Standardized |
| Counterparty Risk | Yes | Minimal (clearinghouse) |
| Settlement | At maturity | Daily (MTM) |

#### C. Contingent Claims (Options)

**Call Option:**
- Right (not obligation) to **buy** underlying at strike price
- **Long call payoff**: Max(0, ST - X)
- **Short call payoff**: -Max(0, ST - X) = Min(0, X - ST)

**Put Option:**
- Right (not obligation) to **sell** underlying at strike price
- **Long put payoff**: Max(0, X - ST)
- **Short put payoff**: -Max(0, X - ST) = Min(0, ST - X)

Where X = Strike price

**Option Moneyness:**
- **In-the-money (ITM)**: Has intrinsic value
  - Call: S > X
  - Put: S < X
- **At-the-money (ATM)**: S = X
- **Out-of-the-money (OTM)**: No intrinsic value
  - Call: S < X
  - Put: S > X

**Option Value Components:**
- **Intrinsic value**: Immediate exercise value
- **Time value**: Value from possibility of favorable price movement

**Factors Affecting Option Value:**
| Factor | Call Value | Put Value |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| ↑ Stock price | ↑ | ↓ |
| ↑ Strike price | ↓ | ↑ |
| ↑ Time to expiration | ↑ | ↑ |
| ↑ Volatility | ↑ | ↑ |
| ↑ Risk-free rate | ↑ | ↓ |
| ↑ Dividends | ↓ | ↑ |

**Put-Call Parity:**
C + PV(X) = P + S

Or: C - P = S - PV(X)

**Binomial Option Pricing:**
- Up and down moves
- Risk-neutral probability
- No-arbitrage pricing

#### D. Swaps

**Swap Definition:**
Agreement to exchange cash flows over multiple periods

**Interest Rate Swap:**
- Exchange fixed interest payments for floating (or vice versa)
- **Plain vanilla swap**: Fixed for floating (e.g., fixed for LIBOR)
- Notional principal not exchanged

**Swap Payoffs:**
- **Pay fixed, receive floating**: Payoff = Floating rate - Fixed rate (× notional)
- **Pay floating, receive fixed**: Payoff = Fixed rate - Floating rate (× notional)

**Currency Swap:**
- Exchange cash flows in different currencies
- Notional principals are exchanged

**Equity Swap:**
- Exchange equity return for fixed/floating rate

### Study Tips
1. **Draw payoff diagrams** - Visualize long/short positions
2. **Understand forwards vs. futures differences**
3. **Master option moneyness and intrinsic value**
4. **Put-call parity is important** - Know the formula
5. **Focus on concepts over complex math** - Level I is mostly qualitative

## Topic 9: Alternative Investments (7-10%)

### Overview
Alternative Investments covers non-traditional asset classes including hedge funds, private equity, real estate, commodities, and infrastructure.

### Key Topics

#### A. Categories of Alternative Investments

**Five Main Categories:**
1. Hedge funds
2. Private capital (private equity, venture capital)
3. Real estate
4. Natural resources (commodities)
5. Infrastructure

#### B. Hedge Funds

**Characteristics:**
- Private investment vehicles
- Limited regulation
- Wide range of strategies
- Performance-based fees (e.g., "2 and 20": 2% management fee + 20% performance fee)
- Lock-up periods
- High minimum investments

**Hedge Fund Strategies:**

1. **Equity Hedge**
   - Long/short equity
   - Market neutral
   - Short bias

2. **Event-Driven**
   - Merger arbitrage
   - Distressed securities
   - Activist strategies

3. **Relative Value**
   - Fixed-income arbitrage
   - Convertible arbitrage

4. **Macro/CTA**
   - Global macro
   - Managed futures

**Hedge Fund Risks:**
- Illiquidity
- Leverage
- Lack of transparency
- Manager risk

#### C. Private Equity

**Characteristics:**
- Investment in private companies or public companies taken private
- Long investment horizon (5-10 years)
- Active management and restructuring
- Exit strategies: IPO, strategic sale, secondary sale

**Types of Private Equity:**

1. **Venture Capital**
   - Early-stage companies
   - High risk, high potential return
   - Stages: Seed, early stage, expansion, later stage

2. **Buyout Funds**
   - Mature companies
   - Often use leverage (LBOs - Leveraged Buyouts)
   - Management buyouts (MBOs)

3. **Growth Capital**
   - Minority investments in established companies

**Private Equity Valuation:**
- Difficult due to lack of market prices
- Methods: Comparables, DCF, recent transaction prices

#### D. Real Estate

**Forms of Real Estate Investment:**

1. **Direct Ownership**
   - Residential, commercial, agricultural

2. **Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)**
   - Publicly traded
   - Required to distribute most income as dividends
   - Equity REITs vs. Mortgage REITs

3. **Private Real Estate Funds**

**Real Estate Investment Characteristics:**
- **Advantages**: Inflation hedge, income generation, diversification
- **Disadvantages**: Illiquidity, high transaction costs, management intensive

**Real Estate Valuation:**
- **Income approach**: Cap rate = NOI / Value
- **Cost approach**: Replacement cost
- **Sales comparison approach**: Comparable transactions

**Real Estate Strategies:**
- **Core**: Stable, income-oriented properties
- **Core-plus**: Some value-add opportunities
- **Value-added**: Requires improvements/repositioning
- **Opportunistic**: High risk, high return, development projects

#### E. Commodities

**Types:**
- Energy (oil, natural gas)
- Metals (gold, silver, copper)
- Agricultural (wheat, corn, soybeans)
- Livestock

**Ways to Invest in Commodities:**
- Physical ownership
- Commodity futures
- Commodity-linked bonds
- Equity in commodity-producing companies

**Characteristics:**
- Inflation hedge
- Portfolio diversification
- No income (except through price appreciation)
- Storage costs (physical commodities)

**Commodity Futures:**
- **Contango**: Futures price > Spot price
- **Backwardation**: Futures price < Spot price
- **Roll yield**: Gain/loss from rolling futures contracts

#### F. Infrastructure

**Types:**
- Transportation (roads, airports, ports)
- Utilities (water, electricity, gas)
- Communication (telecom networks)

**Characteristics:**
- Long-lived assets
- Stable cash flows
- Inflation protection (often regulated with inflation adjustments)
- High barriers to entry
- Capital intensive

### Study Tips
1. **Understand characteristics of each alternative** - How they differ from traditional investments
2. **Know fee structures** - Especially hedge fund fees
3. **Categorize hedge fund strategies** - Equity, event-driven, relative value, macro
4. **Real estate valuation methods** - Cap rate formula
5. **Focus on concepts and characteristics** - Less calculation-heavy than other topics

## Topic 10: Portfolio Management (8-12%)

### Overview
Portfolio Management introduces modern portfolio theory, CAPM, asset allocation, and the investment policy statement. It's the foundation for deeper Level II and III content.

### Key Topics

#### A. Portfolio Risk and Return

**Return Measures:**
- **Holding period return**: (Ending Value - Beginning Value + Income) / Beginning Value
- **Arithmetic mean**: Simple average of returns
- **Geometric mean**: Compound annual growth rate (CAGR)
- **Money-weighted return**: IRR of cash flows
- **Time-weighted return**: Geometric mean of subperiod returns (preferred for performance evaluation)

**Risk Measures:**
- **Variance and standard deviation**
- **Downside risk measures**: Semivariance, target downside deviation
- **Maximum drawdown**
- **Value at Risk (VaR)**

#### B. Portfolio Theory

**Markowitz Portfolio Theory:**
- Investors are risk-averse
- Expected return and risk (standard deviation) are key considerations
- Focus on efficient portfolios

**Portfolio Risk:**
σp² = Σ Σ wi wj Cov(Ri, Rj)

For two assets:
σp² = wA²σA² + wB²σB² + 2wAwBCov(A,B)

Where:
- Cov(A,B) = ρAB × σA × σB

**Diversification:**
- Reduces unsystematic risk (firm-specific risk)
- Cannot reduce systematic risk (market risk)
- Benefits depend on correlation between assets
- Correlation = -1: Perfect negative correlation, maximum diversification
- Correlation = +1: Perfect positive correlation, no diversification

**Minimum Variance Portfolio:**
- Portfolio with lowest risk for given level of assets

**Efficient Frontier:**
- Set of portfolios with maximum return for given risk
- Set of portfolios with minimum risk for given return
- Upward sloping, concave curve

#### C. Capital Market Theory

**Risk-Free Asset:**
- Zero standard deviation
- Known return with certainty
- Examples: T-bills

**Capital Allocation Line (CAL):**
- Combination of risk-free asset and risky portfolio
- Expected Return = Rf + [(E(Rp) - Rf) / σp] × σ
- Slope = Sharpe Ratio = (E(Rp) - Rf) / σp

**Capital Market Line (CML):**
- Special case of CAL using market portfolio
- E(Rp) = Rf + [(E(Rm) - Rf) / σm] × σp
- All investors hold combination of risk-free asset and market portfolio

**Market Portfolio:**
- Portfolio of all risky assets
- Value-weighted
- Mean-variance efficient

#### D. Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)

**CAPM Formula:**
E(Ri) = Rf + βi[E(Rm) - Rf]

Where:
- E(Ri) = Expected return on asset i
- Rf = Risk-free rate
- βi = Beta of asset i
- E(Rm) = Expected return on market
- [E(Rm) - Rf] = Market risk premium

**Beta:**
βi = Cov(Ri, Rm) / σm² = ρi,m × σi / σm

- β > 1: More volatile than market (aggressive)
- β = 1: Same volatility as market
- β < 1: Less volatile than market (defensive)
- β = 0: No systematic risk (risk-free asset)

**Security Market Line (SML):**
- Graphical representation of CAPM
- Plots expected return vs. beta
- All assets should lie on SML in equilibrium

**Using SML:**
- If asset plots **above** SML: **Undervalued** (expected return > required return)
- If asset plots **below** SML: **Overvalued** (expected return < required return)

**CAPM Assumptions:**
- Investors are rational, risk-averse, mean-variance optimizers
- Perfect capital markets (no taxes, no transaction costs)
- All investors have same one-period time horizon
- All investors have homogeneous expectations
- All investments infinitely divisible
- No inflation or interest rate changes

#### E. Systematic vs. Unsystematic Risk

**Total Risk = Systematic Risk + Unsystematic Risk**

**Systematic Risk (Market Risk, Non-diversifiable Risk):**
- Risk affecting entire market
- Cannot be diversified away
- Measured by beta
- Examples: Interest rates, inflation, recessions, wars

**Unsystematic Risk (Firm-Specific Risk, Diversifiable Risk):**
- Risk specific to individual asset or company
- Can be diversified away
- Examples: Management changes, product recalls, strikes, lawsuits

**Well-Diversified Portfolio:**
- Contains many securities
- Unsystematic risk approaches zero
- Only systematic risk remains

#### F. Asset Allocation

**Strategic Asset Allocation:**
- Long-term target allocations
- Based on investor's objectives, constraints
- Reviewed periodically (e.g., annually)

**Tactical Asset Allocation:**
- Short-term deviations from strategic allocation
- Attempt to exploit market opportunities
- Market timing

**Major Asset Classes:**
- Equities (stocks)
- Fixed income (bonds)
- Cash equivalents
- Alternative investments

**Factors Affecting Asset Allocation:**
- Risk tolerance
- Return objectives
- Time horizon
- Liquidity needs
- Tax considerations
- Regulatory/legal constraints

#### G. Investment Policy Statement (IPS)

**Purpose:**
- Document investor's objectives and constraints
- Guide investment decisions
- Establish accountability

**IPS Components:**

**1. Objectives:**
- **Return objectives**: Required return, desired return
- **Risk tolerance**: Ability and willingness to take risk

**2. Constraints:**
- **Time horizon**: Investment period
- **Liquidity**: Cash flow needs
- **Taxes**: Tax situation
- **Legal/regulatory**: Trust laws, regulations
- **Unique circumstances**: Specific restrictions or preferences

**Return Objectives:**
- Absolute return target (e.g., 8% per year)
- Relative return target (e.g., S&P 500 + 2%)

**Risk Objectives:**
- Absolute risk (e.g., standard deviation ≤ 15%)
- Relative risk (e.g., tracking error ≤ 3%)

#### H. Portfolio Execution

**Passive Management:**
- Buy and hold strategy
- Track benchmark/index
- Low cost, low turnover
- Assumes markets are efficient

**Active Management:**
- Attempt to outperform benchmark
- Security selection and market timing
- Higher costs, higher turnover
- Assumes markets are inefficient

**Rebalancing:**
- Adjust portfolio back to target allocation
- Methods: Calendar-based, threshold-based
- Trade-off: Rebalancing costs vs. drift from target

### Study Tips
1. **Master CAPM and SML** - Fundamental to portfolio management
2. **Understand systematic vs. unsystematic risk** - Key concept
3. **Know the Sharpe ratio** - Important performance measure
4. **Practice portfolio risk calculations** - Two-asset portfolios
5. **Memorize IPS components** - Objectives and constraints
6. **Connect to other topics**: Uses QM (statistics, correlation), Equity (returns), Economics

## Level I Study Strategy

### Recommended Study Timeline (4-6 Months)

**Month 1-2: Foundation Topics**
- Quantitative Methods
- Economics
- Ethical and Professional Standards (start early, review throughout)

**Month 3-4: Core Topics**
- Financial Statement Analysis (allow extra time - heavily weighted)
- Corporate Issuers
- Equity Investments
- Fixed Income

**Month 5: Remaining Topics**
- Derivatives
- Alternative Investments
- Portfolio Management

**Month 6: Review and Practice**
- Review all topics
- Mock exams (multiple full-length exams)
- Focus on weak areas
- Final ethics review

### Study Approach

**1. Read/Watch Content**
- Official CFA curriculum or third-party provider
- Take notes on key concepts and formulas

**2. Practice Questions**
- End-of-chapter questions (official curriculum)
- Question bank (third-party)
- Focus on understanding, not just correct answers

**3. Summarize and Review**
- Create formula sheets
- Summarize key concepts
- Review regularly (spaced repetition)

**4. Mock Exams**
- Take at least 3-4 full mock exams
- Simulate real exam conditions
- Review and analyze mistakes thoroughly

### Time Management

**300 Hours Minimum:**
- 50 hours: Ethics
- 40 hours: FSA
- 35 hours: Equity
- 35 hours: Fixed Income
- 30 hours: Quant
- 25 hours: Portfolio Management
- 20 hours: Economics
- 20 hours: Corporate Issuers
- 15 hours: Derivatives
- 15 hours: Alternative Investments
- 15 hours: Mock exams and final review

### Calculator Proficiency

**Approved Calculators:**
- Texas Instruments BA II Plus (most popular)
- Hewlett Packard 12C

**Must-Know Functions:**
- TVM (N, I/Y, PV, PMT, FV)
- NPV and IRR
- Statistical functions (mean, standard deviation)
- Memory functions

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Starting too late** - 300 hours is minimum, not average
2. **Passive reading** - Must practice problems actively
3. **Ignoring weak areas** - Address weaknesses early
4. **Neglecting ethics** - Can make difference in borderline pass/fail
5. **Insufficient mock exams** - Need to practice full exam conditions
6. **Not using calculator efficiently** - Wastes valuable exam time
7. **Memorizing without understanding** - Exam tests application, not recall

### Exam Day Preparation

**One Week Before:**
- Light review of formulas and key concepts
- One final mock exam
- Reduce study intensity (avoid burnout)
- Get adequate sleep

**Day Before:**
- Light review only
- Prepare exam materials (calculator, ID, admission ticket)
- Get good night's sleep

**Exam Day:**
- Arrive early at test center
- Bring valid ID and admission ticket
- Bring approved calculator (with backup)
- Stay calm and pace yourself
- Flag difficult questions, return later
- Use all available time

### Post-Exam

**Pass:**
- Congratulations! Begin preparing for Level II
- Level II is more difficult and requires ~300 hours

**Fail:**
- Don't be discouraged - many successful charterholders failed at least once
- Analyze performance by topic
- Focus on weak areas in next attempt
- Consider different study materials or approach
