# CFA® Level III Comprehensive Study Guide

## Overview of CFA Level III

### Exam Structure
- **Format**: Mix of essay (constructed response) and item set questions
- **Duration**: 4 hours 24 minutes total
- **Two Sessions**: Morning and afternoon
- **Core + Pathway Structure**: 65-70% core + 30-35% specialized pathway

### 2025 Major Change: Specialized Pathways

Starting in 2025, Level III features **three specialized pathways**. Candidates must choose one when registering:

1. **Portfolio Management Pathway**: Traditional institutional portfolio management
2. **Private Wealth Pathway**: High-net-worth individuals and families
3. **Private Markets Pathway**: Alternative investments, private equity, private credit

**Important Notes:**
- The CFA charter is the same regardless of pathway
- Candidates can change pathways if retaking Level III
- Core content (65-70%) is identical for all candidates
- Only the specialized section (30-35%) differs by pathway

### Level III Focus

Level III emphasizes **synthesis and portfolio management**:
- **Application to portfolio management**: Integrate all prior knowledge
- **Asset allocation**: Strategic and tactical decisions
- **Wealth planning**: Individual and institutional investors
- **Risk management**: Comprehensive risk analysis and management
- **Performance evaluation**: Measuring and attributing performance
- **Ethics in practice**: Real-world ethical dilemmas

### Core Topic 2: Asset Allocation (30-35% of Core)

**Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA):**
- Long-term target allocations
- Based on IPS objectives and constraints
- Policy portfolio
- Review periodically (e.g., annually)

**Tactical Asset Allocation (TAA):**
- Short-term deviations from SAA
- Exploit perceived market inefficiencies
- Market timing
- Typically smaller deviations (±5% from SAA)

**Dynamic Asset Allocation:**
- Systematic changes based on age, wealth, liabilities
- Example: Glide path in target-date funds

#### Asset Allocation Approaches

**1. Asset-Only Approach:**
- Focus on asset characteristics only
- Maximize Sharpe ratio
- Mean-variance optimization
- Suitable when liabilities are not well-defined

**2. Liability-Relative Approach:**
- Consider liabilities explicitly
- Surplus optimization: Surplus = Assets - Liabilities
- Suitable for defined benefit pensions, insurance companies
- Goal: Fund liabilities with minimum risk

**3. Goals-Based Approach:**
- Multiple sub-portfolios for different goals
- Each goal has risk/return requirements
- Priority-based funding
- Suitable for individual investors

#### Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO)

**Inputs:**
- Expected returns
- Variances (standard deviations)
- Covariances (correlations)

**Output:**
- Efficient frontier
- Optimal portfolios for each risk level

**Limitations:**
- Highly sensitive to inputs (especially expected returns)
- Concentrated portfolios
- No consideration of liabilities
- Single-period framework

**Improvements:**
- Reverse optimization
- Black-Litterman model
- Resampled efficient frontier
- Risk budgeting

**Black-Litterman Model:**
- Combines market equilibrium with investor views
- Produces more diversified, stable portfolios
- Formula:
  E(R) = [(τΣ)^(-1) + P'ΩP^(-1)]^(-1) × [(τΣ)^(-1)Π + P'Ω^(-1)Q]

Where:
- Π = Equilibrium returns
- P = Matrix linking views to assets
- Q = Investor views
- Ω = Uncertainty in views
- τ = Scaling factor

#### Rebalancing

**Rebalancing Approaches:**
1. **Calendar rebalancing**: Fixed intervals (quarterly, annually)
2. **Percent-range rebalancing**: Rebalance when allocation drifts beyond threshold
3. **Hybrid**: Combine calendar and threshold

**Rebalancing Considerations:**
- **Transaction costs**: Higher costs → wider tolerance bands
- **Risk aversion**: Higher risk aversion → narrower bands
- **Correlation with other assets**: Lower correlation → wider bands
- **Asset class volatility**: Higher volatility → narrower bands
- **Tax considerations**: Taxable accounts → wider bands

**Optimal Rebalancing:**
- Trade-off: Deviation from target vs. rebalancing costs
- Narrower bands for:
  - High risk aversion
  - High return volatility
  - Low transaction costs
  - Low correlation between assets

### Core Topic 3: Portfolio Construction (15-20% of Core)

#### Fixed Income Portfolio Management

**Immunization:**
- Match duration of assets and liabilities
- Protects against parallel yield curve shifts
- **Single liability**: Macaulay duration of portfolio = Liability duration
- **Multiple liabilities**: Match present value and duration

**Immunization Conditions:**
1. PV(Assets) ≥ PV(Liabilities)
2. Duration(Assets) = Duration(Liabilities)
3. Convexity(Assets) > Convexity(Liabilities) (minimize dispersion)
4. Monitor and rebalance as duration drifts

**Cash Flow Matching:**
- Dedicate bond cash flows to meet liability payments
- No immunization risk
- More expensive (lower returns)
- Requires more bonds

**Contingent Immunization:**
- Active management with downside protection
- Establish minimum acceptable terminal value
- If surplus falls to safety net, immunize

**Active Fixed Income Strategies:**

1. **Interest Rate Anticipation**:
   - Increase duration if rates expected to fall
   - Decrease duration if rates expected to rise

2. **Yield Curve Strategies**:
   - **Bullet**: Concentrate maturities
   - **Barbell**: Long and short maturities
   - **Ladder**: Equally spaced maturities

3. **Sector/Credit Strategies**:
   - Rotate among sectors (governments, corporates, MBS)
   - Credit spread trades

4. **Security Selection**:
   - Identify mispriced securities
   - Relative value analysis

**Liability-Driven Investing (LDI):**
- For institutional investors with defined liabilities
- Match or hedge liability characteristics
- Surplus management
- Derivative overlays (interest rate swaps, futures)

#### Equity Portfolio Management

**Passive Management:**
- **Full replication**: Hold all index constituents
- **Stratified sampling**: Representative sample
- **Optimization**: Minimize tracking error
- **Derivatives**: Index futures, ETFs

**Active Management Styles:**

1. **Style Analysis**:
   - Value vs. Growth
   - Large cap vs. Small cap
   - Style box (3x3 grid)

2. **Factor-Based**:
   - Value factor (low P/E, P/B)
   - Size factor (small cap)
   - Momentum factor
   - Quality factor

**Tracking Error:**
- Standard deviation of active returns
- Active return = Portfolio return - Benchmark return
- Tracking error = σ(Active returns)

**Information Ratio:**
IR = Active return / Tracking error

- Measures risk-adjusted active return
- Higher IR = better active management

**Active Share:**
- Measures portfolio differentiation from benchmark
- Active Share = 0.5 × Σ|wi - wi,B|
- Range: 0% (identical to benchmark) to 100% (no overlap)

**Factor Tilt:**
- Passive exposure with systematic factor tilts
- Example: Enhanced indexing with value tilt

### Core Topic 4: Derivatives and Risk Management (15-20% of Core)

#### Currency Risk Management

**Currency Exposure:**
- Direct exposure: Foreign currency assets
- Indirect exposure: Foreign revenues/costs

**Hedging Currency Risk:**

**Forward Contracts:**
- Most common hedging tool
- Lock in exchange rate
- Hedge ratio = 0% (no hedge) to 100% (fully hedged)

**Currency Options:**
- Protection against adverse moves
- Retain upside potential
- Cost: Premium paid

**Strategic Hedging Decision:**
- **Hedge fully** if:
  - Low expected return on foreign currency
  - High currency volatility
  - Short time horizon
- **Hedge partially or not** if:
  - Expected currency appreciation
  - Diversification benefits
  - Long time horizon

**Roll Yield:**
- Gain/loss from rolling forward contracts
- Depends on interest rate differentials
- Forward premium/discount

#### Equity Risk Management

**Beta Management:**
- Adjust portfolio beta using equity futures
- **Number of contracts** = (Target beta - Current beta) × Portfolio value / (Futures price × Multiplier)

**Pre-Investing Cash:**
- Equitize cash using futures
- Synthetic equity exposure
- Avoids trading costs of buying stocks

**Portfolio Insurance:**
- Protect against downside
- Methods:
  1. **Protective put**: Buy put options
  2. **Dynamic hedging**: Adjust stock/cash allocation
  3. **Collars**: Buy put, sell call

**Equity Swaps:**
- Exchange equity returns for fixed/floating rate
- Gain exposure without buying stocks
- Manage tax, regulatory, or diversification issues

#### Fixed Income Risk Management

**Duration Management:**
- Adjust portfolio duration using futures
- **Number of contracts** = -(Target duration - Current duration) × Portfolio value / (Futures duration × Futures price)

**Negative sign**: Short futures to reduce duration

**Yield Curve Risk:**
- Key rate durations
- Partial duration hedging
- Butterfly trades

**Credit Risk Management:**
- Credit default swaps (CDS)
- Buy protection: Pay premium, receive payment if default
- Sell protection: Receive premium, pay if default

#### Portfolio Insurance Strategies

**Protective Put:**
- Buy put option on portfolio
- Floor = Put strike price
- Unlimited upside
- Cost = Put premium

**Covered Call:**
- Sell call option on portfolio
- Capped upside
- Reduced cost (receive premium)

**Collar:**
- Buy put + Sell call
- Defined floor and cap
- Often zero-cost (put premium = call premium)

**Dynamic Hedging (CPPI - Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance):**
- Exposure = M × (Portfolio value - Floor)
- M = Multiplier (typically 2-4)
- Rebalance as portfolio value changes
- Issues: Transaction costs, gap risk

### Core Topic 5: Performance Measurement (5-10% of Core)

#### Return Calculation

**Time-Weighted Return (TWR):**
- Geometric linking of sub-period returns
- Removes effect of cash flows
- Preferred for manager evaluation
- Required by GIPS

**Money-Weighted Return (MWR):**
- IRR of all cash flows
- Includes effect of cash flow timing
- Reflects investor's experience
- Useful for private equity

#### Performance Attribution

**Goal**: Explain sources of portfolio return relative to benchmark

**Asset Allocation Effect:**
- Impact of over/underweighting asset classes
- (wi,P - wi,B) × (Ri,B - RB)

**Security Selection Effect:**
- Impact of security selection within asset class
- wi,B × (Ri,P - Ri,B)

**Interaction Effect:**
- Combined effect
- (wi,P - wi,B) × (Ri,P - Ri,B)

**Fixed Income Attribution:**
- Interest rate effect
- Sector allocation effect
- Security selection effect
- Trading activity effect

#### Risk-Adjusted Performance Measures

**Sharpe Ratio:**
Sharpe = (Rp - Rf) / σp

- Measures excess return per unit of total risk
- Suitable for stand-alone portfolios

**Treynor Ratio:**
Treynor = (Rp - Rf) / βp

- Measures excess return per unit of systematic risk
- Suitable for diversified portfolios

**Jensen's Alpha:**
α = Rp - [Rf + βp(Rm - Rf)]

- Excess return above CAPM required return
- Positive alpha = outperformance

**Information Ratio:**
IR = (Rp - RB) / Tracking error

- Active return per unit of active risk
- Measures manager skill

**M² (M-Squared):**
M² = (Sharpe ratio of portfolio × σBenchmark) + Rf

- Risk-adjusted return on benchmark's risk level
- Easier to interpret than Sharpe ratio

**Appraisal Ratio:**
Appraisal ratio = α / σ(ε)

- Alpha per unit of unsystematic risk

#### GIPS® Standards

**Purpose**: Global standards for performance presentation

**Key Requirements:**
1. **Compliance**: Firm-wide, cannot be selective
2. **Composites**: All actual, fee-paying discretionary portfolios must be in composite
3. **Fair representation and full disclosure**
4. **Verification**: Independent verification (recommended, not required)

**Composite Construction:**
- Group portfolios with similar strategies, objectives
- Include all eligible portfolios
- No cherry-picking

**Performance Presentation:**
- At least 5 years of annual returns (or since inception)
- Eventually 10 years
- Total return
- Composite dispersion
- Number of portfolios in composite
- Composite assets vs. firm assets

## Pathway 1: Portfolio Management (Traditional)

### Focus Areas
- Institutional portfolio management
- Asset allocation
- Manager selection
- Portfolio implementation

### Key Topics

#### Institutional Investor Types

**Defined Benefit Pension Plans:**
- **Characteristics**: Employer bears investment risk
- **Objectives**: Fund liabilities, maximize surplus
- **Risk tolerance**: Depends on funded status, sponsor financial strength
- **Time horizon**: Long term
- **Liquidity**: Generally low, except for mature plans
- **Constraints**: Regulatory (ERISA), tax-exempt

**Defined Contribution Pension Plans:**
- **Characteristics**: Employee bears investment risk
- **Objectives**: Grow retirement savings
- **Risk tolerance**: Varies by participant age, wealth
- **Asset allocation**: Often lifecycle/target-date funds

**Endowments:**
- **Purpose**: Support institutional mission (universities, hospitals)
- **Investment horizon**: Perpetual
- **Spending policy**: Typically 4-5% of assets annually
- **Risk tolerance**: Above average (long horizon)
- **Liquidity**: Low
- **Tax status**: Tax-exempt
- **Unique**: Intergenerational equity

**Foundations:**
- **Private**: Established by individual/family
- **Public**: Funded by public donations
- **Investment horizon**: Perpetual
- **Spending requirement**: 5% minimum (private foundations in US)
- **Similar to endowments**: Long horizon, inflation sensitivity

**Insurance Companies:**

**Life Insurance:**
- **Objectives**: Meet policyholder claims
- **Liabilities**: Long-term, predictable
- **Asset allocation**: Fixed income heavy
- **Risk tolerance**: Below average
- **Liquidity**: Generally predictable
- **Regulatory**: Strict capital requirements

**Non-Life (P&C) Insurance:**
- **Liabilities**: Shorter term, less predictable
- **Asset allocation**: More liquid, shorter duration
- **Tax considerations**: Taxable
- **Underwriting cycle**: Affects risk tolerance

**Sovereign Wealth Funds:**
- **Purpose**: Manage national wealth
- **Types**: Savings, stabilization, development
- **Investment horizon**: Long
- **Risk tolerance**: High (typically)
- **Liquidity**: Generally low

**Banks:**
- **Objectives**: Maximize shareholder value
- **Asset allocation**: Loans, securities
- **Liabilities**: Deposits
- **Risk management**: ALM (Asset-Liability Management)
- **Regulatory**: Basel III capital requirements

#### Manager Selection

**Due Diligence Process:**
1. **Strategy definition**: Define role in portfolio
2. **Manager universe**: Identify potential managers
3. **Preliminary screening**: Quantitative filters
4. **Detailed analysis**: Qualitative and quantitative
5. **Operational due diligence**: Infrastructure, compliance
6. **Final selection**: Best fit for portfolio
7. **Ongoing monitoring**: Performance, style drift

**Quantitative Analysis:**
- Historical returns
- Risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe, IR)
- Tracking error
- Drawdowns
- Style analysis
- Factor exposures

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- Investment philosophy and process
- Personnel (stability, experience)
- Decision-making process
- Firm culture
- Alignment of interests
- Capacity constraints

**Types of Managers:**
- **Active vs. Passive**
- **Core vs. Core-plus**
- **Value vs. Growth**
- **Large, mid, small cap**

#### Portfolio Implementation

**Implementation Choices:**

**1. Direct Investment:**
- Buy individual securities
- Full control
- Higher costs (research, trading)

**2. Pooled Vehicles:**
- **Mutual funds**: Regulated, liquid, transparent
- **ETFs**: Exchange-traded, low cost, tax efficient
- **Commingled funds**: Institutional pooled funds
- **Separate accounts**: Customized, transparent, higher minimums

**3. Derivatives:**
- Futures, swaps, options
- Rapid, cost-effective
- Liquidity management
- Tactical overlay strategies

**Completeness Fund:**
- Complements active managers
- Fills gaps in portfolio
- Minimizes unintended bets
- Passive implementation

**Portable Alpha:**
- Separate alpha generation from beta exposure
- Example: Gain equity market exposure (futures) + alpha from active strategy (hedge fund)
- Total return = Beta return + Alpha

## Pathway 2: Private Wealth

### Focus Areas
- High-net-worth individuals
- Family offices
- Estate planning
- Tax considerations
- Behavioral finance

### Key Topics

#### Individual Investor Types

**Mass Affluent:**
- Net worth $100K - $1M
- Robo-advisors, online platforms
- Standardized solutions

**High Net Worth (HNW):**
- Net worth $1M - $50M
- Personalized advice
- Complex financial situations

**Ultra High Net Worth (UHNW):**
- Net worth $50M+
- Family offices
- Sophisticated strategies
- Multi-generational wealth

#### Investment Policy Statement (IPS) for Individuals

**Return Objectives:**
- **Absolute return**: Target specific return (e.g., 7% annually)
- **Relative return**: Outperform benchmark or inflation
- **Required return**: Based on spending needs, goals
- **Desired return**: Aspirational, may exceed ability

**Risk Objectives:**
- **Ability to take risk**: Financial capacity (wealth, income, time horizon, liabilities)
- **Willingness to take risk**: Psychological tolerance, behavioral factors
- **Overall risk tolerance**: Lower of ability and willingness
- **Measurement**: Standard deviation, shortfall probability, maximum loss

**Constraints:**

**1. Time Horizon:**
- Single-stage vs. multi-stage
- Major life events: Retirement, education funding, inheritance
- Age-based changes

**2. Liquidity:**
- Ongoing expenses
- Emergency reserves (3-6 months)
- One-time needs (home purchase, education)
- Negative liquidity: Inheritances, bonuses

**3. Taxes:**
- Income tax
- Capital gains tax (short-term vs. long-term)
- Estate and gift taxes
- Tax-advantaged accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s)
- Tax-loss harvesting
- Asset location (tax-efficient placement)

**4. Legal and Regulatory:**
- Trust provisions
- Fiduciary standards
- Qualified plan rules
- International regulations

**5. Unique Circumstances:**
- Concentrated stock position
- ESG preferences
- Restrictions on certain investments
- Religious/ethical constraints

#### Life Cycle Investing

**Accumulation Phase:**
- Building wealth
- Long time horizon
- High risk tolerance (ability)
- High equity allocation
- Tax-deferred savings

**Consolidation Phase:**
- Mid-career
- Approaching retirement
- Moderate risk tolerance
- Balanced allocation
- Increase fixed income

**Spending Phase (Decumulation):**
- Retirement
- Living off portfolio
- Lower risk tolerance
- Higher fixed income allocation
- Inflation protection
- Sequence-of-returns risk

**Gifting Phase:**
- Estate planning
- Wealth transfer
- Tax-efficient gifting
- Legacy objectives

#### Concentrated Stock Positions

**Risks:**
- Lack of diversification
- Idiosyncratic risk
- Emotional attachment
- Tax considerations

**Strategies to Reduce Concentration:**

**1. Outright Sale:**
- Simplest approach
- Triggers capital gains tax
- Psychological barriers (loyalty, overconfidence)

**2. Exchange Fund:**
- Pool stock with other investors
- Diversification
- Deferred taxation
- Lockup period (usually 7 years)

**3. Completion Portfolio:**
- Invest remaining wealth to complement concentrated position
- Optimize diversification

**4. Hedging Strategies:**
- **Protective put**: Downside protection, retains upside
- **Covered call**: Income generation, capped upside
- **Collar**: Zero-cost protection, capped upside
- **Prepaid variable forward**: Monetize position, defer tax

**5. Charitable Strategies:**
- **Charitable remainder trust (CRT)**: Income for life, charity gets remainder
- **Donor-advised fund (DAF)**: Immediate tax deduction, flexible giving

#### Behavioral Finance in Wealth Management

**Behavioral Biases:**

**Cognitive Errors** (thinking errors):
- **Anchoring**: Over-reliance on first information
- **Mental accounting**: Treating money differently based on source/use
- **Framing**: Influenced by how choices are presented
- **Availability**: Overweight easily recalled information

**Emotional Biases** (feeling-based):
- **Loss aversion**: Fear of losses > desire for gains
- **Overconfidence**: Overestimate abilities
- **Self-control**: Favoring immediate gratification
- **Status quo**: Preference for no change
- **Endowment**: Overvalue what you own
- **Regret aversion**: Avoid pain of wrong decision

**Goals-Based Investing:**
- Align portfolios with specific goals
- Different risk/return for each goal
- Mental accounting aligned with goals
- Priority-based: Essential, important, aspirational
- Increases client satisfaction and adherence

**Advising Clients:**
- **Moderate** cognitive biases (education, correct thinking)
- **Adapt** to emotional biases (harder to change, work with them)

#### Estate Planning

**Estate Planning Goals:**
- Minimize taxes
- Provide for heirs
- Charitable giving
- Control wealth distribution
- Business succession

**Estate Planning Tools:**

**1. Wills:**
- Specifies asset distribution
- Names guardians for minor children
- Subject to probate

**2. Trusts:**
- **Revocable (living) trust**: Can be changed, avoids probate, no tax benefits
- **Irrevocable trust**: Cannot be changed, removes assets from estate, tax benefits
- **Charitable trusts**: CRT, charitable lead trust (CLT)

**3. Gifting:**
- **Annual exclusion**: Gift up to limit per person per year (tax-free)
- **Lifetime exemption**: Total amount can gift/bequeath tax-free
- **Generation-skipping transfer**: To grandchildren or beyond

**4. Life Insurance:**
- Provide liquidity for estate taxes
- Wealth transfer tool
- Irrevocable life insurance trust (ILIT)

**Estate Tax Strategies:**
- Gifting during lifetime
- Charitable donations
- Trusts
- Marital deduction (unlimited for spouses)
- Family limited partnerships (FLPs)

**Core Capital vs. Excess Capital:**
- **Core capital**: Maintain lifestyle, pass to heirs
- **Excess capital**: Available for philanthropy, aggressive investing

## Pathway 3: Private Markets

### Focus Areas
- Private equity
- Private debt/credit
- Real estate
- Infrastructure
- Hedge funds
- Venture capital

### Key Topics

#### Private Equity

**Types of Private Equity:**

**1. Venture Capital (VC):**
- Early-stage companies
- High risk, high potential return
- Active involvement
- **Stages**: Seed, early stage, expansion, late stage

**2. Buyout Funds:**
- Mature companies
- Leverage (LBOs - Leveraged Buyouts)
- **Types**: Small, mid, large/mega buyouts
- **Strategies**: Management buyouts (MBOs), management buy-ins (MBIs)

**3. Growth Capital:**
- Minority stakes
- Established companies
- Expansion funding

**4. Distressed Investing:**
- Troubled companies
- Bankruptcy/restructuring
- High risk, high return

**Private Equity Fund Structure:**
- **General Partner (GP)**: Manages fund, typically invests 1-3%
- **Limited Partners (LPs)**: Investors
- **Management fees**: 1.5-2.5% of committed capital
- **Carried interest**: 20% of profits above hurdle rate (typically 8%)
- **Hurdle rate**: Minimum return before carry
- **Catch-up**: GP receives higher % to reach 20% total
- **Clawback**: Return carry if early investments overcompensated

**Private Equity Valuation:**
- **Pre-investment**: DCF, multiples (comparable companies, transactions)
- **During holding**: Adjusted for developments, milestones
- **Exit**: Sale price, IPO price

**Challenges:**
- Infrequent valuation (quarterly, annual)
- Lack of market prices
- Smoothed returns (stale pricing)
- Survivorship bias

**Private Equity Performance Measurement:**
- **IRR (Money-weighted return)**: Appropriate due to capital calls and distributions
- **TVPI (Total Value to Paid-In)**: Distributed value + Residual value / Paid-in capital
- **DPI (Distributed to Paid-In)**: Cash-on-cash return
- **RVPI (Residual Value to Paid-In)**: Unrealized value / Paid-in capital
- TVPI = DPI + RVPI

**J-Curve:**
- Early years: Negative returns (fees, no exits)
- Later years: Positive returns (exits, value creation)
- Cumulative return negative initially, then positive

#### Real Estate

**Forms of Real Estate Investment:**

**1. Private/Direct:**
- Direct ownership
- Control
- Illiquid
- High minimums

**2. Public REITs:**
- Liquid
- Diversified
- Professional management
- Required to distribute ≥90% of taxable income

**3. Private Real Estate Funds:**
- Commingled funds
- Institutional investors
- Less liquid than REITs

**Real Estate Strategies:**
- **Core**: Stable, high-quality, fully leased, low leverage
- **Core-Plus**: Some value-add opportunities, moderate leverage
- **Value-Added**: Requires improvements, higher leverage, higher return target
- **Opportunistic**: Development, repositioning, high leverage, highest return target

**Real Estate Valuation:**

**1. Income Approach:**
Cap Rate = NOI / Value

Value = NOI / Cap rate

Where NOI = Net Operating Income (rental income - operating expenses)

**2. Cost Approach:**
Value = Land value + (Replacement cost - Depreciation)

**3. Sales Comparison:**
Value based on comparable transactions

**Real Estate Due Diligence:**
- Location analysis
- Property condition
- Lease terms (length, renewal options, escalations)
- Tenant quality
- Market supply/demand
- Environmental issues
- Title and legal

#### Hedge Funds

**Hedge Fund Strategies:**

**1. Equity Strategies:**
- **Long/short equity**: Long undervalued, short overvalued
- **Market neutral**: Beta ≈ 0, pure alpha
- **Short bias**: Net short exposure

**2. Event-Driven:**
- **Merger arbitrage**: Long target, short acquirer
- **Distressed securities**: Bankrupt/restructuring companies
- **Activist**: Influence management/strategy

**3. Relative Value:**
- **Fixed income arbitrage**: Exploit bond pricing inefficiencies
- **Convertible arbitrage**: Long convertible bond, short stock
- **Statistical arbitrage**: Quantitative, mean reversion

**4. Macro:**
- **Global macro**: Top-down, currencies, rates, commodities
- **Managed futures (CTA)**: Trend-following, systematic

**Hedge Fund Characteristics:**
- Limited regulation
- Sophisticated investors
- Performance-based fees ("2 and 20")
- Use of leverage
- Short selling
- Derivatives
- Lock-up periods
- High minimums

**Hedge Fund Due Diligence:**
- **Investment due diligence**: Strategy, process, performance, risk
- **Operational due diligence**: Organization, valuation, controls, legal/compliance

**Hedge Fund Risk Measures:**
- Sharpe ratio
- Maximum drawdown
- Downside deviation
- Value at Risk (VaR)
- Conditional VaR (CVaR)

#### Infrastructure

**Types of Infrastructure:**
- **Economic infrastructure**: Transportation (roads, airports), utilities (water, power), communication
- **Social infrastructure**: Schools, hospitals, prisons

**Investment Characteristics:**
- **Long-lived assets**: 30-100 year life
- **Stable cash flows**: Often regulated or contracted
- **Inflation protection**: Revenue linked to inflation
- **Capital intensive**: Large upfront investment
- **Barriers to entry**: Natural monopolies, high costs

**Greenfield vs. Brownfield:**
- **Greenfield**: New construction, higher risk/return
- **Brownfield**: Existing, operational, lower risk/return

**Infrastructure Risks:**
- Regulatory/political risk
- Construction risk (greenfield)
- Usage/demand risk
- Financial risk (leverage)

### Essay Question Strategy

**CFA Institute Command Words:**
- **Calculate**: Compute numerical answer, show work
- **Compare/Contrast**: Describe similarities and differences
- **Demonstrate**: Show understanding through example
- **Describe**: Provide characteristics, features
- **Determine**: Reach decision, conclusion
- **Discuss**: Analyze, explain implications
- **Distinguish**: Point out differences
- **Evaluate**: Assess, judge
- **Explain**: Give reasons, make clear
- **Formulate**: Develop, create
- **Identify**: Name, specify
- **Interpret**: Explain meaning
- **Justify**: Give reasons in support
- **Recommend**: Advise specific action
- **State**: Express main points

**Writing Tips:**
1. **Answer the question asked**: Read carefully, address all parts
2. **Be concise**: Get to the point, avoid filler
3. **Use structured format**: Numbered lists, bullet points
4. **Show your work**: Calculations with steps
5. **Write legibly**: Graders must read it
6. **Time management**: Don't spend too long on one question
7. **Use templates**: IPS format, return calculation format

**Template Approach:**
- Develop templates for common questions (IPS, return requirements, risk objectives)
- Practice applying templates
- Adapt to specific question

**Time Management:**
- Read question carefully (2-3 minutes)
- Plan answer (1-2 minutes)
- Write answer (remaining time)
- Move on if stuck (flag, return later)

## Key Formulas and Concepts Summary

### Asset Allocation
- Sharpe Ratio = (Rp - Rf) / σp
- Information Ratio = Active return / Tracking error
- Resampled efficient frontier, Black-Litterman

### Fixed Income
- Duration matching for immunization
- Cash flow matching
- Convexity desirable for immunization

### Derivatives
- Number of futures contracts = (Target - Current) × Value / (Futures × Multiplier)
- Equity beta adjustment, duration adjustment, currency hedging

### Performance
- TWR vs. MWR
- Attribution: Allocation + Selection + Interaction
- Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen's Alpha, M²

### Private Markets
- PE: IRR, TVPI, DPI, RVPI, J-curve
- Real Estate: Cap rate = NOI / Value
- Hedge funds: 2 and 20, absolute return
