## Code of Ethics

### Six Fundamental Principles

All CFA Institute members and candidates must:

**1. Act with integrity, competence, diligence, and respect**
- Integrity: Honest, trustworthy, principled
- Competence: Knowledge and skills to perform duties
- Diligence: Careful and thorough
- Respect: Professional courtesy

**2. Place integrity of the investment profession and interests of clients above their own interests**
- Clients come first
- Profession's reputation matters
- No self-dealing

**3. Use reasonable care and exercise independent professional judgment when conducting investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, and engaging in other professional activities**
- Think independently
- Due diligence required
- Resist undue pressure

**4. Practice and encourage others to practice in a professional and ethical manner that will reflect credit on themselves and the profession**
- Lead by example
- Encourage ethical behavior in others
- Mentor junior professionals

**5. Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society**
- Markets exist to serve society
- Fair, efficient markets benefit everyone
- Prevent fraud and manipulation

**6. Maintain and improve their professional competence and strive to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals**
- Lifelong learning
- Keep skills current
- Share knowledge
- Continuing education

## Standards of Professional Conduct

### Standard I: Professionalism

#### I(A) Knowledge of the Law

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must understand and comply with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations (including the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct) of any government, regulatory organization, licensing agency, or professional association governing their professional activities.

In the event of conflict, Members and Candidates must comply with the more strict law, rule, or regulation.

Members and Candidates must not knowingly participate or assist in and must dissociate from any violation of such laws, rules, or regulations.

**Guidance:**
- Know laws of jurisdictions where you practice
- Comply with most strict requirement
- Do not knowingly violate or assist violations
- Dissociate from violations

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Stay informed of applicable laws and regulations
- Review firm procedures
- Maintain current reference materials
- Seek advice from compliance or legal counsel
- Document dissociation from violations
- Report violations to appropriate authorities if required

**Violations:**
- Trading while aware of material nonpublic information
- Ignoring or assisting in violation of securities laws
- Failing to dissociate from known violations

**Dissociation:**
- Remove name from written materials
- Request different assignment
- Document disagreement in writing
- Escalate to supervisor or compliance
- Resign if necessary (extreme cases)

#### I(B) Independence and Objectivity

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must use reasonable care and judgment to achieve and maintain independence and objectivity in their professional activities.

Members and Candidates must not offer, solicit, or accept any gift, benefit, compensation, or consideration that reasonably could be expected to compromise their own or another's independence and objectivity.

**Guidance:**
- Maintain independence in analysis and recommendations
- Distinguish fact from opinion
- Avoid conflicts of interest
- Gifts/entertainment: Token items OK, material value not OK

**Common Threats:**
- Issuer-paid research
- Investment banking pressure
- Buy-side pressure on sell-side analysts
- Gifts, entertainment, travel
- Allocation of IPO shares
- Performance-based compensation tied to specific outcomes

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Firm policies on gifts and entertainment
- Restricted lists for personal trading
- Firewalls/information barriers
- Reporting relationships (research separate from banking)
- Flat fees for research (not success fees)
- Disclose conflicts in research reports

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Analyst changes rating to secure investment banking business
- **Violation**: Accept lavish gifts from company being covered
- **Not a violation**: Accept modest gift (pen, coffee mug) under $100
- **Not a violation**: Corporate-sponsored travel if customary (e.g., site visit)

#### I(C) Misrepresentation

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must not knowingly make any misrepresentations relating to investment analysis, recommendations, actions, or other professional activities.

**Guidance:**
- No omission of relevant facts
- No false or misleading statements
- Distinguish facts from opinions
- Attribute sources properly (avoid plagiarism)
- Cherry-picking data prohibited
- Marketing materials must be fair and balanced

**Types of Misrepresentation:**
1. **Plagiarism**: Using someone else's work without attribution
2. **Selective disclosure**: Omitting unfavorable information
3. **Inflated claims**: Exaggerating abilities or results
4. **Misleading charts**: Manipulated scales, cherry-picked dates
5. **False credentials**: Claiming degrees, certifications not earned

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Verify information before distribution
- Cite sources appropriately
- Review marketing materials for accuracy
- Keep records of research and analysis
- Use phrase "CFA candidate" only if registered, not after failing or withdrawing

**CFA Designation Usage:**
- **Correct**: "Jane Doe, CFA" or "Jane Doe, Chartered Financial Analyst"
- **Incorrect**: "Jane Doe, C.F.A." or "Doe, cfa" (wrong punctuation/case)
- **Incorrect**: "Jane Doe is a Chartered Financial Analyst" (not a noun)
- **Candidate**: Only if currently registered for next exam

#### I(D) Misconduct

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must not engage in any professional conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, or deceit or commit any act that reflects adversely on their professional reputation, integrity, or competence.

**Guidance:**
- Applies to professional conduct
- Dishonesty, fraud, deceit prohibited
- Conduct reflecting poorly on profession prohibited
- Personal conduct may be covered if impacts professional reputation

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Falsifying documents (resumé, expense reports)
- **Violation**: DUI conviction (reflects poorly on character)
- **Violation**: Sexual harassment
- **Not a violation**: Personal bankruptcy (if not from fraud)
- **Not a violation**: Civil disputes unrelated to professional conduct

### Standard II: Integrity of Capital Markets

#### II(A) Material Nonpublic Information

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates who possess material nonpublic information that could affect the value of an investment must not act or cause others to act on the information.

**Definitions:**
- **Material**: Information that would impact investment decision or price
- **Nonpublic**: Not disseminated to marketplace, not available to general public

**Guidance:**
- Do not trade on MNPI
- Do not tip others
- Do not cause others to trade
- Exercise care in handling confidential information

**Mosaic Theory:**
- Material public + Non-material nonpublic + Analysis = **Not a violation**
- Piecing together public and non-material information through diligent analysis is permitted
- Key: No individual piece of nonpublic information is material

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Establish information barriers (Chinese walls)
- Restricted lists for trading
- Watch lists
- Physical separation of departments
- Review employee trading
- Prohibit interdepartmental communications without clearance

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Company executive tells analyst earnings will miss; analyst trades/recommends
- **Violation**: Overhear merger discussion in elevator; trade on information
- **Not a violation**: Piece together public info + channel checks + analysis to predict earnings miss
- **Not a violation**: Trade on public information (press release, SEC filing)

#### II(B) Market Manipulation

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must not engage in practices that distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume with the intent to mislead market participants.

**Types of Manipulation:**
1. **Price manipulation**: Transactions intended to distort prices
2. **Volume manipulation**: Trading to give false impression of activity
3. **Information-based manipulation**: Spreading false information

**Guidance:**
- No pump and dump schemes
- No painting the tape
- No wash trades, matched orders
- No spreading false rumors
- Legitimate trading strategy not a violation

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Buy stock while spreading false positive rumors; sell at inflated price
- **Violation**: Execute offsetting trades to inflate volume
- **Violation**: Trade to move price to target for options expiration
- **Not a violation**: Large block trades that move price (legitimate)
- **Not a violation**: Expressing informed opinion (distinguished from rumor)

### Standard III: Duties to Clients

#### III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates have a duty of loyalty to their clients and must act with reasonable care and exercise prudent judgment.

Members and Candidates must act for the benefit of their clients and place their clients' interests before their employer's or their own interests.

**Guidance:**
- Client's interests come first
- Fiduciary duty
- Understand client's needs, circumstances
- Exercise prudent judgment
- Duty to employer subordinate to duty to client (but still exists)

**Identifying the Client:**
- Individual account: The individual
- Pension fund: Plan participants (not company)
- Mutual fund: Fund shareholders (not fund company)
- Trust: Beneficiaries (not trustee/grantor)

**Soft Dollars:**
- Use client commissions only for direct client benefit
- Research and execution services acceptable
- Overhead, office rent, travel not acceptable
- Full disclosure required

**Proxy Voting:**
- Duty to vote proxies in client's best interest
- Have policy and disclose
- Cost-benefit analysis acceptable

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Written policies on conflicts
- Regular account reviews
- Client disclosure and consent
- Duty of loyalty policy

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Recommend higher commission product with no benefit to client
- **Violation**: Use client commissions to pay for office rent
- **Not a violation**: Use client commissions for research benefiting clients

#### III(B) Fair Dealing

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must deal fairly and objectively with all clients when providing investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, or engaging in other professional activities.

**Guidance:**
- Treat all clients fairly
- Do not discriminate or favor certain clients
- Disseminate recommendations fairly
- Disclose level of service provided
- Different service levels acceptable if disclosed

**Investment Recommendations:**
- Disseminate simultaneously to all clients
- If not possible, establish fair procedures
- Pre-dissemination must be limited, brief
- Family accounts treated like client accounts

**Investment Actions:**
- Allocate trades fairly
- Pro rata allocation most common
- Block trades and average pricing
- Document allocation procedures
- IPO allocation policies

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Limit pre-dissemination (need-to-know only)
- Simultaneous dissemination
- Tiered service levels disclosed
- Trade allocation procedures
- Supervisory review of allocations

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Inform favorite clients of rating change before others
- **Violation**: Allocate profitable trades to favored accounts
- **Not a violation**: Different service levels if disclosed upfront
- **Not a violation**: Trading for personal account after clients executed

#### III(C) Suitability

**The Standard:**
When Members and Candidates are in an advisory relationship with a client, they must:
1. Make a reasonable inquiry into a client's or prospective client's investment experience, risk and return objectives, and financial constraints prior to making any investment recommendation or taking investment action and must reassess and update this information regularly.
2. Determine that an investment is suitable to the client's financial situation and consistent with the client's written objectives, mandates, and constraints before making an investment recommendation or taking investment action.
3. Judge the suitability of investments in the context of the client's total portfolio.

**Guidance:**
- Know your client (KYC)
- Understand IPS: objectives and constraints
- Regular updates required
- Consider total portfolio context
- Different standards: advisory vs. execution-only

**IPS Components:**
- **Objectives**: Return, risk
- **Constraints**: Liquidity, time horizon, taxes, legal/regulatory, unique

**Unsolicited Trades:**
- If advisory relationship: Must still be suitable
- If execution-only: Less stringent (but know basics)
- Warn if appears unsuitable

**Compliance Procedures:**
- IPS for each client
- Periodic IPS updates
- Document suitability analysis
- Supervisory review

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Recommend aggressive strategy to retiree needing income
- **Violation**: Recommend leveraged ETF without understanding risks
- **Not a violation**: Execute unsolicited trade (execution-only relationship) with warning

#### III(D) Performance Presentation

**The Standard:**
When communicating investment performance information, Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that it is fair, accurate, and complete.

**Guidance:**
- Present performance fairly and accurately
- Include all relevant information
- No cherry-picking accounts
- Disclose calculation methodology
- Present benchmarks
- GIPS compliance recommended

**Requirements:**
- Historical performance must be complete
- Include benchmark comparison
- Disclose whether gross or net of fees
- Explain calculation methodology
- Acknowledge limitations (e.g., simulated, model, back-tested)

**What Not to Do:**
- Cherry-pick best accounts
- Show only profitable periods
- Exclude cash drag
- Use misleading benchmarks
- Survivor bias (exclude closed accounts)

**Compliance Procedures:**
- GIPS® compliance (best practice)
- Composites of similar accounts
- Written performance presentation policies
- Include disclosures

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Show only top 10% of accounts
- **Violation**: Show performance from favorable start date only
- **Not a violation**: GIPS-compliant composite presentation

#### III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must keep information about current, former, and prospective clients confidential unless:
1. The information concerns illegal activities on the part of the client or prospective client,
2. Disclosure is required by law, or
3. The client or prospective client permits disclosure of the information.

**Guidance:**
- Maintain client confidentiality
- Applies to current, former, and prospective clients
- Exceptions: Illegal activity, legal requirement, client permission

**Illegal Activity Exception:**
- Consult counsel and supervisor
- May report to authorities
- CFA Institute Standards do not require reporting (but law may)

**Legal Requirement:**
- Court order, subpoena
- Regulatory investigation
- Comply with law

**Electronic Communication:**
- Encrypted email
- Secure file sharing
- Care on social media
- Proper disposal of documents

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Shred sensitive documents
- Encrypted electronic communication
- Limit access to confidential information
- Firewalls between departments

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Discuss client's financial situation at cocktail party
- **Violation**: Use former client's information without permission
- **Not a violation**: Disclose to regulator when required by law
- **Not a violation**: Disclose with client's written permission

### Standard IV: Duties to Employers

#### IV(A) Loyalty

**The Standard:**
In matters related to their employment, Members and Candidates must act for the benefit of their employer and not deprive their employer of the advantage of their skills and abilities, divulge confidential information, or otherwise cause harm to their employer.

**Guidance:**
- Duty of loyalty to current employer
- Do not harm employer's interests
- No misuse of confidential information
- Independent practice OK with disclosure and consent
- Preparation for departure is permitted (but limitations)

**Leaving an Employer:**
- Cannot take client lists (if employer's property)
- Cannot solicit clients until after departure
- Can inform clients of departure
- Cannot take confidential records
- May plan new business (but cannot implement until after leaving)

**Whistleblowing:**
- Violation of employer's interest may be OK if reporting illegal activity
- Consult counsel
- Follow firm procedures

**Independent Practice:**
- Obtain written consent from employer
- Disclose nature and duration
- Disclose compensation arrangement

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Written compliance policies
- Acknowledge employer policies
- Procedures for independent practice
- Exit interviews

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Solicit clients while still employed
- **Violation**: Take employer's client lists
- **Violation**: Misuse employer's resources for personal business
- **Not a violation**: Inform clients you are leaving (factual)
- **Not a violation**: Plan business model before leaving (but don't solicit)

#### IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must not accept gifts, benefits, compensation, or consideration that competes with or might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with their employer's interest unless they obtain written consent from all parties involved.

**Guidance:**
- Disclose to employer in writing
- Obtain written consent from employer
- Applies to compensation from clients or others
- Gifts must not conflict with employer's interest

**What to Disclose:**
- Amount and nature of compensation
- Services to be provided
- Duration of arrangement

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Written disclosure and consent
- Update when arrangement changes
- Supervisory procedures

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Accept bonus from client for favorable report without disclosing to employer
- **Violation**: Accept consulting fees without employer consent
- **Not a violation**: Accept compensation after written disclosure and consent

#### IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that anyone subject to their supervision or authority complies with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and the Code and Standards.

**Guidance:**
- Establish compliance system
- Adequate supervision
- Cannot delegate supervisory responsibility
- Respond to violations

**Adequate Compliance System:**
- Written compliance procedures
- Proper dissemination
- Assignment of responsibilities
- Regular monitoring and updates
- Disciplinary procedures

**Responding to Violations:**
- Investigate thoroughly
- Increase supervision
- Limit activities until resolved
- Impose sanctions (suspension, termination)
- Report to appropriate authorities if required

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Compliance manual
- Training programs
- Hire qualified compliance staff
- Clear lines of authority and responsibility
- Adequate resources for compliance
- Regular compliance audits

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Ignore evidence of employee violations
- **Violation**: Fail to implement adequate compliance procedures
- **Not a violation**: Violation occurs despite adequate system and supervision

### Standard V: Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions

#### V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must:
1. Exercise diligence, independence, and thoroughness in analyzing investments, making investment recommendations, and taking investment actions.
2. Have a reasonable and adequate basis, supported by appropriate research and investigation, for any investment analysis, recommendation, or action.

**Guidance:**
- Thorough analysis required
- Degree of diligence depends on:
  - Complexity of investment
  - Role and function
  - Available resources
- Distinguish facts from opinions
- Secondary research acceptable if sound
- Group research: Individual responsibility remains

**Third-Party Research:**
- May use if reasonable basis to trust provider
- Review provider's methodology
- Consider independence and objectivity
- Cannot blindly rely

**Quantitative Models:**
- Understand assumptions and limitations
- Test models before use
- Monitor performance
- Update as needed

**Group Research:**
- May rely on others' work
- Must understand basis
- Individual is responsible for own recommendations

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Establish research standards
- Review requirements
- Document research and analysis
- Maintain research files

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Recommend stock without analysis
- **Violation**: Rely on rumor or unverified sources
- **Not a violation**: Use third-party research after evaluating provider
- **Not a violation**: Update analysis as new information emerges

#### V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must:
1. Disclose to clients and prospective clients the basic format and general principles of the investment processes they use to analyze investments, select securities, and construct portfolios and must promptly disclose any changes that might materially affect those processes.
2. Disclose to clients and prospective clients significant limitations and risks associated with the investment process.
3. Use reasonable judgment in identifying which factors are important to their investment analyses, recommendations, or actions and include those factors in communications with clients and prospective clients.
4. Distinguish between fact and opinion in the presentation of investment analyses and recommendations.

**Guidance:**
- Explain investment process
- Disclose limitations and risks
- Include relevant factors
- Separate facts from opinions
- Use clear, concise language

**Format and Process:**
- General description OK (not every detail)
- Disclose material changes
- Explain philosophy and approach

**Limitations and Risks:**
- Model limitations
- Liquidity constraints
- Leverage risks
- Currency risk
- Soft data or forward-looking estimates

**Opinions:**
- Clearly label as opinion
- Distinguish from facts
- Support with reasoning

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Develop written disclosures
- Fact vs. opinion guidelines
- Review marketing materials
- Update disclosures when processes change

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Fail to disclose model limitations
- **Violation**: Present opinion as fact
- **Not a violation**: Omit proprietary details of model
- **Not a violation**: Disclose general approach and material factors

#### V(C) Record Retention

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must develop and maintain appropriate records to support their investment analyses, recommendations, actions, and other investment-related communications with clients and prospective clients.

**Guidance:**
- Retain records supporting analysis and recommendations
- Maintain for at least 7 years (or per law/regulation)
- Electronic and written records
- If employer doesn't retain, member/candidate should

**Types of Records:**
- Investment analyses and reports
- Meeting notes
- Emails and correspondence
- Trade blotters
- Models and assumptions
- Research reports used

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Establish retention policies
- Designate responsibility
- Organized filing system
- Secure storage
- Electronic backups

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Fail to maintain records of investment analysis
- **Violation**: Discard records before minimum period
- **Not a violation**: Maintain only summary of voluminous data if reasonable

### Standard VI: Conflicts of Interest

#### VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity or interfere with respective duties to their clients, prospective clients, and employer.

Members and Candidates must ensure that such disclosures are prominent, are delivered in plain language, and communicate the relevant information effectively.

**Guidance:**
- Disclose all conflicts
- Prominent and plain language
- Ensure client understands
- Update as conflicts change
- Disclosure alone may not be sufficient

**Types of Conflicts:**
- Ownership of securities being recommended
- Directorships
- Investment banking relationships
- Referral arrangements
- Fee structures
- Family relationships

**Disclosure Methods:**
- Verbal (not sufficient alone)
- Written (preferred)
- Regulatory filings
- Prospectus, marketing materials

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Conflict identification procedures
- Standard disclosure language
- Regular updates
- Cross-departmental communication
- Review of personal holdings

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Recommend stock without disclosing ownership
- **Violation**: Fail to disclose that firm has banking relationship with issuer
- **Not a violation**: Full disclosure of conflicts in research report

#### VI(B) Priority of Transactions

**The Standard:**
Investment transactions for clients and employers must have priority over investment transactions in which a Member or Candidate is the beneficial owner.

**Guidance:**
- Clients first
- Employer (firm accounts) second
- Personal accounts last
- Family accounts treated as personal
- No front-running

**Trading for Personal Account:**
- After clients have had opportunity
- Disclose personal holdings to employer
- Obtain pre-clearance if required
- Blackout periods for IPOs, private placements

**Exceptions:**
- IPOs, private placements generally prohibited for personal accounts
- Automatic investment plans (if not timed to exploit information)

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Pre-clearance requirements
- Duplicate confirms and statements to firm
- Blackout periods
- Restricted lists
- Reporting requirements

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Buy for personal account before entering client orders
- **Violation**: Trade ahead of research report release
- **Not a violation**: Trade for personal account after clients executed

#### VI(C) Referral Fees

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must disclose to their employer, clients, and prospective clients, as appropriate, any compensation, consideration, or benefit received from or paid to others for the recommendation of products or services.

**Guidance:**
- Disclose referral fees received
- Disclose referral fees paid
- Disclose to clients and employer
- Before or at time of recommendation
- Allows client to evaluate bias

**What to Disclose:**
- Nature of consideration (cash, soft credits)
- Estimated dollar value
- Nature of relationship

**Compliance Procedures:**
- Written disclosure
- Track all referral arrangements
- Update as arrangements change

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Receive referral fee from attorney without disclosing to client
- **Violation**: Pay referral fee to broker without client knowledge
- **Not a violation**: Full disclosure of referral arrangement

### Standard VII: Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or CFA Candidate

#### VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs

**The Standard:**
Members and Candidates must not engage in any conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the CFA designation or the integrity, validity, or security of CFA Institute programs.

**Guidance:**
- No cheating on exam
- No sharing exam content
- Do not disclose confidential program information
- Report violations

**Prohibited Conduct:**
- Cheating on exam
- Revealing exam questions
- Improperly using CFA designation
- Misrepresenting credentials
- Violating exam policies

**Compliance:**
- Understand exam policies
- Report suspected violations
- Proper use of designation

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: Discuss specific exam questions after exam
- **Violation**: Bring prohibited materials into exam
- **Not a violation**: Discuss general topics covered (no specific questions)

#### VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program

**The Standard:**
When referring to CFA Institute, CFA Institute membership, the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA Program, Members and Candidates must not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or implications of membership in CFA Institute, holding the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA Program.

**Guidance:**
- Do not overstate meaning of designation
- Cannot say you are "CFA certified"
- Cannot imply superior performance
- Proper punctuation and spelling

**Proper Use of CFA Designation:**
- "John Doe, CFA" ✓
- "John Doe, Chartered Financial Analyst" ✓
- "John Doe, C.F.A." ✗ (periods not allowed)
- "John Doe is a Chartered Financial Analyst" ✗ (not a noun)

**CFA Candidacy:**
- "Jane Smith, CFA Level II Candidate" ✓
- "Jane Smith, CFA Candidate" ✓
- Only if actively registered for next exam
- Cannot claim candidacy after failing/withdrawing

**Prohibited Statements:**
- "Passed CFA Level I" (before receiving results)
- "CFA certification" (it's a charter/designation, not certification)
- "Guaranteed to achieve superior results"
- Implying CFA charterholders achieve better performance

**Compliance:**
- Review marketing materials
- Educate staff on proper use
- Use CFA Institute guidelines

**Examples:**
- **Violation**: "CFA certification ensures superior returns"
- **Violation**: Add periods: "C.F.A."
- **Not a violation**: "Jane Doe, CFA" or "CFA Level II Candidate" (if registered)

## Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS®)

### Overview of GIPS

**Purpose:**
- Global standards for calculating and presenting investment performance
- Fair representation and full disclosure
- Comparability of performance across firms
- Increase confidence in investment industry

**Key Principles:**
1. Fair representation
2. Full disclosure
3. Firm-wide compliance
4. Voluntary

### Compliance Requirements

**Who Can Claim Compliance:**
- Investment management firms only (not individuals, plans, or consultants)
- Must comply with all requirements
- Must be verified (recommended but not required)

**Fundamental Principles:**
- Total return
- Time-weighted returns (TWR)
- Composites of similar strategies
- GIPS-compliant presentation

**Composites:**
- Group portfolios with similar strategy, objectives, inception date
- Include all actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolios
- No cherry-picking
- Apply prospectively (new portfolios into appropriate composite)
- Define composites based on strategy

### Performance Presentation Requirements

**Compliant Presentation Must Include:**
- At least 5 years of annual performance (or since firm inception if less)
- Eventually 10 years
- Total return for each year
- Benchmark return
- Composite assets and % of firm assets
- Number of portfolios in composite
- Dispersion measure
- Percentage of firm assets represented
- Three-year ex-post standard deviation

**Returns:**
- Gross-of-fees and/or net-of-fees
- Time-weighted returns
- Include cash and equivalents
- Subtract trading expenses
- Treat large external cash flows consistently

**Benchmark:**
- Appropriate for strategy
- Disclosed in presentation
- Cannot be blend of composites

**Verification:**
- Independent verification recommended
- Verification is firm-wide
- Does not guarantee accuracy of specific composite

### Advertising Guidelines

**GIPS Compliance in Ads:**
- Can state "GIPS-compliant"
- List composites and descriptions available on request
- Cannot claim GIPS compliance for individual portfolio

**Compliance Statement:**
"[Firm Name] claims compliance with the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS®) and has prepared and presented this report in compliance with the GIPS standards. [Firm Name] has been independently verified for the periods [dates]."

## Application Framework

### Approach to Ethics Questions

**Step-by-Step Process:**

1. **Identify the facts**
   - Who are the parties involved?
   - What happened?
   - When did it happen?

2. **Identify relevant Standards**
   - Which Standards apply?
   - Are there multiple Standards involved?

3. **Analyze the situation**
   - Was there a violation?
   - What should have been done?
   - What are recommended procedures?

4. **Make a decision**
   - Comply with the Standard
   - Consider priority of duties (Clients > Employer > Profession)
   - Recommend actions

### Priority of Duties

When conflicts arise:
1. **Clients** (highest priority in most cases)
2. **Employer**
3. **Investment Profession/Markets**
4. **Self** (lowest priority)

**Example:** If employer asks you to violate client's interests, you must refuse and protect client.

### Mosaic Theory

**Definition:** Combining material public information with non-material nonpublic information through diligent analysis to reach investment conclusion.

**Requirements:**
- No piece of nonpublic information is material by itself
- Reach conclusion through analysis
- Diligent research

**Not a Violation**: Even if conclusion is "material" information, if derived through legitimate mosaic approach.

## Common Scenarios and Violations

### Scenario 1: Material Nonpublic Information

**Situation:** You overhear two executives discussing an upcoming merger.

**Violation:** II(A) Material Nonpublic Information

**Action:**
- Do not trade
- Do not tip others
- Consult compliance
- Seek public confirmation
- Document

### Scenario 2: Soft Dollars

**Situation:** Portfolio manager uses client commissions to pay for office rent.

**Violation:** III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care

**Correct Approach:**
- Use soft dollars only for research and execution
- Must benefit clients directly
- Full disclosure

### Scenario 3: Front-Running

**Situation:** Analyst buys stock for personal account before issuing buy recommendation.

**Violation:** VI(B) Priority of Transactions

**Correct Approach:**
- Clients trade first
- Personal trading after clients have opportunity
- Pre-clearance procedures

### Scenario 4: Cherry-Picking Performance

**Situation:** Firm shows only best-performing accounts in marketing.

**Violation:** III(D) Performance Presentation

**Correct Approach:**
- Use composites (all accounts with similar strategy)
- GIPS compliance
- Show complete history

### Scenario 5: Misrepresenting CFA Designation

**Situation:** "John is a CFA certified analyst."

**Violation:** VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute

**Correct:** "John Doe, CFA" or "John Doe, Chartered Financial Analyst"

### Scenario 6: Independence Threat

**Situation:** Company threatens to stop doing investment banking business unless analyst raises rating.

**Violation of:** I(B) Independence and Objectivity (if analyst caves)

**Correct Approach:**
- Maintain independent opinion
- Report pressure to supervisor/compliance
- Establish firewalls

## Study Tips and Strategies

### General Study Tips

1. **Read the Standards of Practice Handbook**
   - Primary source material
   - Free from CFA Institute
   - Read, don't skim

2. **Practice with scenarios**
   - Ethics is application, not memorization
   - Do all end-of-chapter questions
   - Blue box examples

3. **Study throughout prep**
   - Don't save ethics for last
   - Review periodically
   - Ethics appears at all 3 levels

4. **Understand the "why"**
   - Rationale behind Standards
   - Not just what's prohibited, but why

5. **Know recommended procedures**
   - Not just violations
   - Compliance and prevention

### Exam Day Tips

**Read Carefully:**
- Details matter
- "Most appropriate," "least likely"
- All facts are relevant

**Identify Violations:**
- May be multiple per scenario
- Note all Standards involved

**Prioritize:**
- Clients > Employer > Profession
- More serious violation takes precedence

**Common Traps:**
- Gift value threshold: token (<$100) OK, material not OK
- Dissociation: must be documented, visible
- Disclosure: not always sufficient (may need consent)
- Mosaic: all pieces of MNPI must be non-material

- Fair dealing
- Loyalty to clients vs. employer
- Independence and objectivity
- Suitability
- Performance presentation
- Proper use of designation

- **Clients first**: Above employer, above self
- **Disclosure**: Often required but not always sufficient
