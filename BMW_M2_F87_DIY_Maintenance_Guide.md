## Tools & Equipment Needed

### Essential Basic Tools

**Socket & Wrench Sets:**
- Metric socket set (8mm-24mm)
- 3/8" and 1/2" drive ratchets
- Extension bars (3", 6", 12")
- Metric combination wrench set

**Specialized Sockets:**
- 36mm oil filter housing socket OR oil filter housing wrench tool
- 14mm thin-wall 12-point spark plug socket (deep well)
- 17mm hex socket (for transmission/diff drain plugs)

- **Hydraulic floor jack** (minimum 3-ton capacity)

**General Tools:**
- Pliers set

## Safety First

### Critical Safety Rules

**NEVER:**
- Work under vehicle supported only by jack
- Use damaged or inadequate jack stands
- Exceed tool/equipment weight ratings
- Work on hot engine (especially coolant system)
- Smoke near fuel or brake fluid
- Mix different brake fluid types

**ALWAYS:**
- Use jack stands (redundant support preferred)
- Work on level, solid surface
- Engage parking brake
- Use wheel chocks
- Wear safety glasses
- Have fire extinguisher nearby
- Work in well-ventilated area
- Dispose of fluids properly

### Vehicle Support Points

**Recommended Jack Points:**
- **Front:** Reinforced pinch weld points (marked with notches)
  - OR: Front subframe crossmember
- **Rear:** Reinforced pinch weld points
  - OR: Differential mounting point

**Jack Stand Points:**
- Place on pinch welds OR subframe
- Ensure stable, flat contact
- Test stability before working under vehicle

## Oil Change Procedure

**Difficulty:** Easy
**Time Required:** 30-45 minutes
**Interval:** 10,000 miles or 12 months

### Required Parts & Fluids

| Item | Part Number | Quantity | Cost |
|------|-------------|----------|------|
| Engine Oil (5W-30 LL-01) | 83 21 2 365 925 | 7 quarts | $50-70 |
| Oil Filter | 11 42 7 848 321 | 1 | $15-25 |
| Drain Plug Washer | 11 13 7 548 533 | 1 | $1-2 |
| Oil Filter Housing O-Rings | In filter kit | 1 set | Included |

**Alternative Oils:**
- Mobil 1 FS 0W-40 European Car Formula
- Castrol Edge Professional LL01 5W-30
- Liqui Moly Leichtlauf High Tech LL 5W-30
- Pennzoil Platinum Euro 5W-30

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Warm engine to operating temperature (easier oil drainage)
2. Park on level surface
3. Engage parking brake
4. Gather tools and materials

**Step 1: Lift Vehicle**
1. Loosen wheel lug bolts (19mm socket) - don't remove yet
2. Jack up front of vehicle
3. Place jack stands under pinch welds or subframe
4. Lower vehicle onto stands
5. Remove front wheels (optional, provides better access)

**Step 2: Drain Oil**
1. Locate oil drain plug (bottom of oil pan, driver's side)
2. Position drain pan under drain plug
3. Remove drain plug with 17mm socket
   - ⚠️ **Caution:** Oil may be hot!
4. Allow oil to drain completely (10-15 minutes)
5. Clean drain plug and pan surface
6. Install new copper crush washer on drain plug
7. Reinstall drain plug
   - **Torque:** 25 Nm (18 lb-ft)
   - Don't overtighten!

**Step 3: Remove Oil Filter**
1. Locate oil filter housing (top of engine, passenger side)
2. Position drain pan under housing (oil will drip)
3. Use 36mm socket or filter housing tool
4. Turn counterclockwise to loosen
5. Remove housing and filter assembly
6. Let oil drain from housing

**Step 4: Install New Filter**
1. Remove old filter from housing
2. Clean housing thoroughly
3. Inspect O-rings (should be in new filter kit)
4. Install new filter in housing
5. Lubricate O-rings with fresh oil
6. Install new O-rings on housing
7. Hand-tighten housing into engine
8. **Torque:** 25 Nm (18 lb-ft)
   - ⚠️ **Do not overtighten** - plastic housing can crack!

**Step 5: Fill with New Oil**
1. Lower vehicle (or leave raised)
2. Open oil filler cap (top of engine, driver's side)
3. Insert funnel
4. Pour in 6.5 liters (6.9 quarts) of fresh oil
5. Reinstall oil filler cap

**Step 6: Check for Leaks**
1. Start engine
2. Let idle for 1-2 minutes
3. Check under vehicle for leaks at drain plug and filter housing
4. Shut off engine
5. Wait 5 minutes for oil to settle

**Step 7: Check Oil Level**
1. Remove dipstick (orange loop, driver's side)
2. Wipe clean
3. Reinsert fully
4. Remove and check level
5. Should be between MIN and MAX marks
6. Add oil if needed (typically not necessary)

**Step 8: Reset CBS**
1. Use iDrive or OBD tool to reset oil service indicator
2. (See CBS Reset section for detailed instructions)

**Step 9: Final Checks**
1. Reinstall wheels if removed
   - **Torque:** 140 Nm (103 lb-ft) in star pattern
2. Lower vehicle
3. Recheck oil level
4. Check for leaks
5. Take short test drive
6. Recheck oil level and leaks after drive

**Disposal:**
- Take used oil and filter to auto parts store or recycling center
- Most accept for free

### Pro Tips

- Use Fumoto valve (quick drain valve) for easier oil changes
- Magnetic drain plug catches metal particles
- Oil analysis (Blackstone Labs) helps determine optimal change interval
- Keep oil change log with mileage and date

## Brake Pad Replacement

**Difficulty:** Moderate
**Time Required:** 1.5-2 hours (all 4 wheels)
**Interval:** 30,000-70,000 miles (varies by driving style)

### Required Parts & Tools

**Parts:**
- Front brake pads: 34 11 6 797 859 (OEM)
- Rear brake pads: 34 21 6 876 157 (OEM)
- Brake cleaner spray
- Anti-seize compound

**Tools:**
- C-clamp or brake caliper piston tool
- 7mm hex socket (caliper guide pins)
- Wire brush
- Brake pad wear sensor (if equipped - replace when replacing pads)

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Check brake pad thickness
   - Minimum: 3mm
   - Replace if at or below 4mm
2. Gather tools and parts

**Step 1: Lift Vehicle & Remove Wheel**
1. Loosen lug bolts (don't remove)
2. Jack up vehicle
3. Place on jack stands
4. Remove wheel completely

**Step 2: Inspect Brake System**
1. Inspect rotors for:
   - Cracks
   - Deep grooves
   - Minimum thickness (Front: 28mm, Rear: 22mm)
2. Inspect brake lines and hoses
3. Check caliper for leaks

**Step 3: Remove Caliper** (Front or Rear)

**Front Brakes:**
1. Remove brake pad wear sensor (if equipped)
   - Press tab, pull out
2. Remove lower caliper guide pin bolt
   - 7mm hex socket
3. Swing caliper up on upper guide pin
4. Remove old brake pads
   - May need to pry out
5. Hang caliper with wire or bungee
   - ⚠️ **Don't let hang by brake line!**

**Rear Brakes:**
1. Remove wear sensor (if equipped)
2. Remove caliper guide pin bolts
   - Both bolts (7mm hex)
3. Remove caliper
4. Remove old pads
5. Hang caliper safely

**Step 4: Compress Caliper Piston**
1. Open brake fluid reservoir cap slightly (releases pressure)
2. Use C-clamp or caliper tool
3. Compress piston(s) fully into caliper
   - Fluid level in reservoir will rise
   - Don't overflow reservoir

**Step 5: Clean & Inspect**
1. Clean caliper bracket with wire brush
2. Remove old pad hardware clips
3. Clean guide pins
4. Apply thin layer of anti-seize to guide pins
5. Install new hardware clips (if included with pads)

**Step 6: Install New Pads**
1. Install inner and outer pads
   - Ensure proper orientation
   - Tabs should fit into caliper bracket
2. Some pads have directional arrows

**Step 7: Reinstall Caliper**
1. Lower caliper over new pads
2. Align guide pin holes
3. Apply small amount of anti-seize to guide pin threads
4. Install guide pin bolts
   - **Torque:** ~30 Nm (22 lb-ft) - hand-tight plus
   - Don't overtighten
5. Reconnect brake wear sensor

**Step 8: Repeat for Other Wheels**
- Follow same procedure for remaining 3 wheels

**Step 9: Pump Brake Pedal**
1. Before driving, pump brake pedal 10-15 times
2. Pedal should feel firm
3. If soft, check for air in system (may need bleeding)

**Step 10: Test & Bed-In**
1. Test brakes at low speed before driving
2. Perform proper bed-in procedure:
   - **10-15 stops** from 50 mph to 30 mph (moderate braking)
   - **5-10 stops** from 60 mph to 20 mph (harder braking)
   - **Cool-down period:** Drive without braking for 5 minutes
   - Avoid complete stops during bed-in if possible
3. Allows pad material to transfer evenly to rotors
4. Critical for preventing brake judder

**Step 11: Final Checks**
1. Check brake fluid level (top off if needed)
2. Close reservoir cap
3. Reinstall wheels
   - Torque to 140 Nm (103 lb-ft)
4. Lower vehicle
5. Recheck lug bolt torque
6. Test drive in safe area

### Pro Tips

- Replace all 4 wheels at once for even braking
- Consider upgrading to performance pads (Hawk, EBC, Pagid)
- Apply small amount of copper anti-seize to pad backing plates (prevents squeal)
- Don't touch pad friction surface with bare hands (oils reduce performance)
- If rotors are near minimum thickness, replace with pads

## Air Filter Replacement

**Difficulty:** Very Easy
**Time Required:** 10-15 minutes
**Interval:** 30,000 miles (sooner in dusty conditions)

### Required Parts

**Engine Air Filter:**
- Part Number: 13 71 7 571 355
- **Alternative:** K&N 33-2997 (washable/reusable)

**Cabin Air Filter:**
- Part Number: 64 31 9 272 528 (standard)
- Part Number: 64 31 9 313 519 (activated charcoal)

### Engine Air Filter Replacement

**Step 1: Locate Air Box**
- Driver's side of engine bay
- Black plastic box

**Step 2: Open Air Box**
1. Release clips on sides of air box (usually 4-6 clips)
2. May have screws (T25 Torx)
3. Lift top of air box

**Step 3: Remove Old Filter**
1. Pull old filter straight up
2. Inspect for dirt, damage

**Step 4: Clean Air Box**
1. Wipe inside of air box with clean rag
2. Remove any debris

**Step 5: Install New Filter**
1. Insert new filter
2. Ensure proper seating (edges sealed)
3. Close air box lid
4. Re-fasten clips/screws

### Cabin Air Filter Replacement

**Step 1: Locate Cabin Filter**
- Behind glove box

**Step 2: Remove Glove Box** (on some models)
1. Open glove box
2. Press sides inward to release stops
3. Lower glove box down

**Step 3: Access Filter Housing**
1. Locate rectangular filter housing
2. Release clips or tabs

**Step 4: Remove Old Filter**
1. Slide filter out (note orientation - usually arrow showing airflow direction)
2. May be very dirty

**Step 5: Install New Filter**
1. Insert new filter (match airflow direction arrow)
2. Close housing
3. Reinstall glove box

**Pro Tip:**
- K&N washable engine air filter saves money long-term
- Clean K&N filter every 50,000 miles
- Cabin filter replacement improves HVAC performance and air quality

## Spark Plug Replacement

**Difficulty:** Moderate
**Time Required:** 1.5-2 hours
**Interval:** 30,000-45,000 miles (turbocharged N55)

### Required Parts & Tools

**Parts:**
- NGK 97506 SILZKBR8D8S spark plugs (quantity: 6)
- Ignition coils (if replacing): 12 13 8 616 153 (quantity: 6)
- Anti-seize compound

**Tools:**
- 14mm thin-wall 12-point spark plug socket (deep well)
- 3/8" or 1/2" drive ratchet with extensions
- Torque wrench
- Small flathead screwdriver (to release coil connectors)
- Compressed air or shop vac (to clean spark plug wells)

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Engine should be COLD
2. Disconnect battery negative terminal (optional but recommended)

**Step 1: Remove Engine Cover**
1. Pull up on engine cover
2. Should release from rubber mounts
3. Set aside

**Step 2: Remove Ignition Coils**
1. Locate ignition coils (6 total, on top of engine)
2. Disconnect electrical connector
   - Press tab and pull straight up
3. Remove coil mounting bolt (10mm socket)
4. Pull coil straight up with twisting motion
   - May be tight
5. Set coils aside in order (note which cylinder each came from)

**Step 3: Clean Spark Plug Wells**
1. **Critical step** - prevents dirt from falling into cylinders
2. Use compressed air to blow out each well
3. OR use shop vac to suck out debris
4. Ensure wells are completely clean

**Step 4: Remove Old Spark Plugs**
1. Use 14mm thin-wall 12-point spark plug socket
2. Insert socket fully onto spark plug
3. Turn counterclockwise to loosen
4. Once loose, remove by hand
5. Inspect old plugs for:
   - Wear patterns
   - Damage
   - Unusual deposits (indicates engine issues)
6. Repeat for all 6 cylinders

**Step 5: Gap New Spark Plugs** (if needed)
1. NGK 97506 come pre-gapped
2. **Do not adjust gap** - iridium tip is fragile
3. If using different plugs, gap to specification
4. BMW spec: Check specific to your plugs

**Step 6: Install New Spark Plugs**
1. Apply small amount of anti-seize to threads
   - ⚠️ **Very light coating only**
   - Keep away from electrode
2. Thread spark plug by hand first
   - Ensures not cross-threading
3. Tighten with socket
   - **Torque:** 23 Nm (17 lb-ft)
   - ⚠️ **Do not overtighten!**
4. Repeat for all 6 cylinders

**Step 7: Reinstall Ignition Coils**
1. Insert coil into spark plug well
2. Ensure coil seats fully
3. Install coil mounting bolt
   - Snug but don't overtighten
4. Reconnect electrical connector
   - Should click into place
5. Repeat for all 6 cylinders

**Step 8: Reinstall Engine Cover**
1. Align cover with rubber mounts
2. Press down firmly until seated

**Step 9: Reconnect Battery** (if disconnected)

**Step 10: Test**
1. Start engine
2. Should start smoothly
3. Listen for misfires (rough running)
4. Check for error codes (if OBD scanner available)

**Step 11: Test Drive**
1. Short test drive
2. Engine should run smooth
3. No misfires
4. Improved throttle response

### Interpreting Old Spark Plugs

**Normal:**
- Light tan/gray deposits
- Slight electrode wear

**Carbon Fouled:**
- Black, sooty deposits
- Indicates rich condition or oil burning

**Oil Fouled:**
- Wet, oily deposits
- Indicates oil entering cylinder (valve seals, rings)

**Overheating:**
- White, blistered electrode
- Indicates too lean condition or timing issues

### Pro Tips

- Replace ignition coils if any have failed
- If one coil fails, others often follow soon
- Many owners replace all coils at 60,000-80,000 miles as preventive measure
- NGK is OEM supplier - high quality
- Write mileage on each coil with paint pen for future reference

## Brake Fluid Flush

**Difficulty:** Moderate
**Time Required:** 1-2 hours
**Interval:** 2 years or 30,000 miles

### Required Parts & Tools

**Fluid:**
- BMW Brake Fluid DOT 4 LV (83 13 2 405 977)
- OR ATE TYP 200 DOT 4
- OR Motul RBF 600 (performance/track)
- **Quantity:** 1 liter

**Tools:**
- Brake bleeder kit OR vacuum bleeder
- 11mm wrench (bleeder nipples)
- Clear tubing
- Catch bottle
- Turkey baster or fluid pump (to remove old fluid from reservoir)

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Park on level surface
2. Gather tools and fresh fluid
3. One person can do this with vacuum bleeder
4. Two-person method preferred

**Step 1: Remove Old Fluid from Reservoir**
1. Locate brake fluid reservoir (driver's side, near firewall)
2. Clean area around cap
3. Remove cap
4. Use turkey baster or pump to remove old fluid
5. Dispose of old fluid properly
6. Wipe reservoir clean
7. Fill with fresh fluid to MAX line

**Step 2: Prepare for Bleeding**
1. Lift vehicle and remove all 4 wheels (easier access)
2. Locate bleeder nipples on each caliper
   - Rear of each caliper
   - Cover with rubber cap
3. Have assistant sit in driver's seat

**Step 3: Bleeding Sequence**
- **Order:** RR → LR → RF → LF (rear right to left front)
- Furthest from master cylinder first

**Step 4: Bleed Each Caliper (Two-Person Method)**

**At Each Wheel:**
1. Remove rubber cap from bleeder nipple
2. Clean bleeder nipple
3. Attach clear tubing to bleeder
4. Place other end of tubing in catch bottle
5. **Assistant:** Pump brake pedal 5-10 times, hold down
6. **Bleeder:** Open bleeder nipple 1/2 turn with 11mm wrench
   - Fluid/air will flow through tube
7. **Assistant:** Pedal will go to floor - keep holding
8. **Bleeder:** Close bleeder nipple before pedal released
9. **Assistant:** Pump pedal again
10. Repeat until fluid runs clear (no air bubbles)
    - 5-10 cycles typical
11. Close bleeder nipple
12. Reinstall rubber cap

**After each wheel:**
- Check reservoir level
- Top off if needed (never let run empty!)

**Step 5: Vacuum Bleeder Method** (One Person)
1. Attach vacuum bleeder to nipple
2. Open bleeder nipple 1/2 turn
3. Apply vacuum
4. Fluid will be sucked through
5. When fluid runs clear, close bleeder
6. Repeat for each wheel (same sequence)

**Step 6: Final Checks**
1. Top off reservoir to MAX line
2. Reinstall cap
3. Pump brake pedal 10-20 times
4. Pedal should feel firm (not spongy)
5. If spongy, repeat bleeding process

**Step 7: Test**
1. Reinstall wheels
   - Torque: 140 Nm (103 lb-ft)
2. Lower vehicle
3. Test brakes at low speed before driving
4. Full test drive
5. Verify firm pedal

### Pro Tips

- Use pressure bleeder for easiest one-person operation
- Brake fluid absorbs moisture - use fresh fluid from sealed container
- Don't reuse old brake fluid
- Brake fluid damages paint - wipe spills immediately
- Motul RBF 600 has higher boiling point (best for track)
- Some M2s have brake fluid level sensor - may trigger warning during bleeding
- Clear new vs. old fluid:
  - New: Light yellow, clear
  - Old: Dark brown/amber

## Differential Fluid Change

**Difficulty:** Moderate
**Time Required:** 30-45 minutes
**Interval:** 40,000-50,000 miles

### Required Parts & Tools

**Fluid:**
- BMW SAF-XO 75W-140 (83 22 0 440 214)
- Quantity: 1 liter
- Alternative: Redline 75W-140 or Motul Gear 300 LS 75W-90 (use caution with alternatives)

**Tools:**
- 14mm hex socket (fill and drain plugs)
- Fluid transfer pump
- Drain pan
- New drain plug washers (crush washers)

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Warm differential (drive 10-15 minutes)
   - Warm fluid drains easier
2. Park on level surface

**Step 1: Lift Rear of Vehicle**
1. Jack up rear
2. Place on jack stands
3. Ensure vehicle is level front-to-back

**Step 2: Locate Fill and Drain Plugs**
- **Fill plug:** Side of differential housing (higher)
- **Drain plug:** Bottom of differential

**Step 3: Remove Fill Plug First**
1. ⚠️ **Critical:** Remove fill plug BEFORE drain plug
2. Ensures you can refill after draining
3. 14mm hex socket
4. May be very tight (use breaker bar if needed)
5. Set aside with washer

**Step 4: Drain Old Fluid**
1. Position drain pan under differential
2. Remove drain plug with 14mm hex
3. Let drain completely (5-10 minutes)
4. Inspect old fluid:
   - Metal shavings (normal, some OK)
   - Excessive metal or debris (problem)

**Step 5: Clean and Reinstall Drain Plug**
1. Clean drain plug and differential surface
2. Inspect magnetic drain plug for metal particles
3. Install new copper/aluminum crush washer
4. Reinstall drain plug
   - **Torque:** 45 Nm (33 lb-ft)

**Step 6: Fill with New Fluid**
1. Attach fluid transfer pump to fluid bottle
2. Insert pump hose into fill hole
3. Pump fluid into differential
4. Fill until fluid reaches bottom of fill hole
   - Fluid will start to seep out
5. May take 0.8-1.0 liters

**Step 7: Reinstall Fill Plug**
1. Install new crush washer on fill plug
2. Reinstall fill plug
   - **Torque:** 45 Nm (33 lb-ft)

**Step 8: Clean and Check**
1. Wipe differential housing clean
2. Lower vehicle
3. Test drive
4. Check for leaks

**Step 9: Disposal**
- Take old gear oil to recycling center

### Pro Tips

- Use magnetic drain plug to catch metal particles
- Slight amount of metal shavings is normal
- If excessive metal: Differential may be wearing
- Check for leaks at seals while underneath
- Some owners use Redline Shockproof or Royal Purple - research before using non-OEM
- M Variable LSD requires specific fluid - don't use generic gear oil

## Manual Transmission Fluid Change

**Difficulty:** Moderate
**Time Required:** 45-60 minutes
**Interval:** 50,000-60,000 miles

### Required Parts & Tools

**Fluid:**
- BMW MTF-LT-5 (83 22 2 282 671)
- Quantity: 1.7-2.0 liters
- **Alternatives:**
  - Redline MT-90 75W-90
  - Motul Gear 300 75W-90
  - Royal Purple Synchromax

**Tools:**
- 17mm hex socket (fill and drain plugs)
- Fluid transfer pump
- Drain pan
- New drain plug washers

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Warm transmission (drive 10-15 minutes)
2. Park on level surface

**Step 1: Lift Vehicle**
1. Jack up front of vehicle
2. Place on jack stands
3. May need to remove undertray for access

**Step 2: Locate Fill and Drain Plugs**
- Transmission is below engine, driver's side
- **Fill plug:** Side of transmission (higher)
- **Drain plug:** Bottom of transmission

**Step 3: Remove Fill Plug First**
1. ⚠️ **Critical:** Fill plug first
2. 17mm hex socket
3. May be very tight
4. Set aside with washer

**Step 4: Drain Old Fluid**
1. Position drain pan
2. Remove drain plug with 17mm hex
3. Let drain completely (10-15 minutes)
4. Old fluid may be dark - normal

**Step 5: Clean and Reinstall Drain Plug**
1. Clean plug and transmission surface
2. Install new crush washer
3. Reinstall drain plug
   - **Torque:** 60 Nm (44 lb-ft)

**Step 6: Fill with New Fluid**
1. Use fluid transfer pump
2. Insert hose into fill hole
3. Pump fluid into transmission
4. Fill until fluid reaches bottom of fill hole
5. Takes approximately 1.7 liters

**Step 7: Reinstall Fill Plug**
1. Install new crush washer
2. Reinstall fill plug
   - **Torque:** 60 Nm (44 lb-ft)

**Step 8: Test**
1. Lower vehicle
2. Test drive
3. Shift through all gears
4. Check for leaks

**Step 9: Settle and Recheck** (Optional)
1. After 100 miles, some owners top off fluid
2. Small amount may settle
3. Usually not necessary

### Pro Tips

- Many owners prefer Redline MT-90 over OEM fluid (smoother shifts)
- Experiment with different fluids to find what you prefer
- Cold shift quality may improve with fluid change
- Track cars should change every 30,000 miles

## Battery Replacement & Registration

**Difficulty:** Easy (replacement) / Easy (registration with right tool)
**Time Required:** 30 minutes
**Interval:** 4-6 years

### Required Parts & Tools

**Battery:**
- Group 49 or H8 AGM battery
- 900+ CCA, 90-92 Ah
- Examples:
  - OEM BMW AGM: 61 21 7 604 822
  - Bosch S5 AGM
  - Interstate MTX-H8

**Tools:**
- 10mm socket (battery terminals)
- 13mm socket (hold-down bracket)
- Battery terminal cleaner
- **OBD tool** for registration (BimmerCode, Carly, INPA)

### Step-by-Step Procedure

**Step 1: Prepare**
1. Ensure all accessories off
2. Windows up
3. Doors unlocked (some lock when battery disconnected)

**Step 2: Remove Old Battery**
1. Open hood
2. Locate battery (passenger side, front)
3. Remove battery cover if equipped
4. Disconnect negative terminal first (black, -)
   - 10mm socket
5. Disconnect positive terminal (red, +)
   - 10mm socket
6. Remove hold-down bracket
   - 13mm socket
7. Lift battery out (heavy - ~50 lbs)

**Step 3: Clean Battery Tray**
1. Clean battery tray
2. Remove corrosion
3. Neutralize acid (baking soda + water)
4. Dry thoroughly

**Step 4: Install New Battery**
1. Place new battery in tray
2. Ensure proper orientation (+ terminal toward driver's side)
3. Install hold-down bracket
4. Connect positive terminal first (+)
   - Tighten with 10mm socket
5. Connect negative terminal (-)
   - Tighten with 10mm socket
6. Apply terminal protector spray

**Step 5: Register Battery** (CRITICAL STEP)

**Why Registration is Necessary:**
- BMW power management system tracks battery age
- Adjusts charging profile
- Failure to register causes premature battery failure

**Using BimmerCode/Carly:**
1. Connect OBD adapter to port (under dash, driver's side)
2. Open app on phone
3. Navigate to "Service Functions" or "Battery"
4. Select "Battery Registration"
5. Enter battery specifications:
   - Type: AGM
   - Capacity: 90 Ah (or specific to your battery)
   - Manufacturer (optional)
6. Confirm registration

**Using INPA (PC software):**
1. Connect INPA cable
2. Run INPA software
3. Select appropriate module (usually DME/DDE)
4. Navigate to battery registration
5. Input battery data
6. Write to vehicle

**Step 6: Verify**
1. Start vehicle
2. Check for battery warning lights
3. Check charging voltage (should be 13.5-14.5V)
4. All systems should function normally

**Step 7: Disposal**
- Return old battery to auto parts store
- Usually $10-20 core charge refund

### Pro Tips

- **ALWAYS register new battery** - not optional on BMW
- If changing between AGM and Lead-Acid, must CODE the change (not just register)
- Battery tender for cars stored long periods
- AGM batteries last longer than Lead-Acid
- Check battery voltage regularly (12.6V+ when off)

## CBS (Condition Based Service) Reset

**Difficulty:** Easy (with tool)
**Time Required:** 5 minutes
**When:** After performing service (oil, brakes, etc.)

### Methods to Reset CBS

**Method 1: iDrive (Built-In)**

1. Turn ignition on (don't start engine)
2. Press iDrive knob
3. Navigate to "Vehicle Information" or "Service"
4. Select "Service Required"
5. Select service to reset (Oil, Brakes, etc.)
6. Press and hold BC button on turn signal stalk
   - OR use iDrive to select "Reset"
7. Confirm reset

**Method 2: OBD Tool (BimmerCode, Carly)**

1. Connect OBD adapter
2. Open app
3. Navigate to "Service" or "CBS"
4. Select service to reset
5. Reset
6. Confirm

**Method 3: Dealer**
- Any BMW dealer can reset CBS for free
- May be combined with other service

### Services That Can Be Reset

- **Oil**: After oil change
- **Brake fluid**: After brake fluid flush
- **Brake pads**: After pad replacement (front and rear separate)
- **Vehicle inspection**: Annual inspection
- **Microfilter**: Cabin air filter
- **Spark plugs**: After replacement

- Oil and filter change
